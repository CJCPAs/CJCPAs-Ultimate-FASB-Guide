# ASC 705: Cost of Sales and Services

> Guidance on recognizing and presenting costs directly associated with generating revenue.

## Overview

ASC 705 provides guidance on accounting for costs of sales and services, including the classification and presentation of costs directly attributable to revenue-generating activities. This topic addresses when and how entities should recognize costs as cost of goods sold or cost of services.

> **Core Principle:** Costs of sales and services should be recognized in the same period as the related revenue, ensuring proper matching of expenses with the revenues they help generate.

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Cost of goods sold (COGS)
- Cost of services rendered
- Costs directly attributable to revenue-generating activities
- Consideration received from vendors

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Selling, general, and administrative expenses
- Research and development costs (ASC 730)
- Inventory measurement (ASC 330)
- Long-term contract costs (ASC 606)

---

## Key Concepts

### Cost of Goods Sold Components

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Direct materials** | Raw materials and components used in production |
| **Direct labor** | Wages of workers directly involved in manufacturing |
| **Manufacturing overhead** | Indirect costs allocated to production |
| **Freight-in** | Shipping costs to acquire inventory |
| **Purchasing costs** | Costs of acquiring inventory |

### Cost of Services Components

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Direct labor** | Wages of service providers |
| **Direct materials** | Materials consumed in providing service |
| **Subcontractor costs** | Third-party service costs |
| **Allocated overhead** | Indirect costs related to service delivery |

---

## Classification and Presentation

### Income Statement Presentation

**Gross Profit Model:**
```
Revenue                           $1,000,000
Cost of Sales                       (600,000)
                                  ----------
Gross Profit                        $400,000
Operating Expenses                  (250,000)
                                  ----------
Operating Income                    $150,000
```

### What to Include in Cost of Sales

| Include | Exclude |
|---------|---------|
| Direct product costs | Selling expenses |
| Manufacturing overhead | General & administrative |
| Inbound freight | Outbound shipping (unless policy) |
| Warehousing (production) | Warehousing (distribution) |
| Depreciation (production assets) | Depreciation (non-production) |

## Consideration Received from Vendors

### ASC 705-20: Key Guidance

When a customer receives consideration from a vendor, it should generally be treated as a **reduction of the cost of goods purchased** unless:

1. It's payment for distinct goods or services, OR
2. An identifiable benefit can be measured at fair value

### Types of Vendor Consideration

| Type | Treatment |
|------|-----------|
| **Volume rebates** | Reduce cost of inventory/COGS |
| **Slotting fees** | Generally reduce cost of inventory |
| **Cooperative advertising** | Reduce cost unless identifiable benefit |
| **Promotional allowances** | Reduce cost of goods purchased |
| **Cash discounts** | Reduce cost of inventory |

### Journal Entry—Volume Rebate

**Scenario:** Retailer earns $50,000 rebate from vendor based on purchase volume.

```
Dr. Accounts Receivable (or Cash)         $50,000
    Cr. Inventory (or COGS if sold)              $50,000
```

### Timing of Recognition

**Rebates should be recognized when:**
- Probable that rebate will be earned
- Amount can be reasonably estimated

**If not probable or estimable:**
- Recognize when earned or received

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Manufacturing Company COGS

**Cost Flow for Product:**

| Cost Element | Amount |
|--------------|--------|
| Raw materials used | $200,000 |
| Direct labor | $150,000 |
| Manufacturing overhead | $100,000 |
| **Total manufacturing costs** | **$450,000** |
| Add: Beginning WIP | $30,000 |
| Less: Ending WIP | $(40,000) |
| **Cost of goods manufactured** | **$440,000** |
| Add: Beginning finished goods | $80,000 |
| Less: Ending finished goods | $(70,000) |
| **Cost of goods sold** | **$450,000** |

### Example 2: Service Company Cost of Services

**Professional Services Firm:**

| Cost Element | Amount |
|--------------|--------|
| Professional staff salaries | $500,000 |
| Subcontractor fees | $100,000 |
| Direct materials | $20,000 |
| Travel (client-related) | $30,000 |
| **Cost of services** | **$650,000** |

### Example 3: Vendor Rebate with Inventory

**Scenario:** Company purchases $1,000,000 of inventory. Vendor offers 5% rebate if purchases exceed $800,000.

**At Purchase:**
```
Dr. Inventory                           $1,000,000
    Cr. Accounts Payable                       $1,000,000
```

**When Rebate Threshold Met:**
```
Dr. Accounts Receivable                    $50,000
    Cr. Inventory                                 $50,000
```

**If inventory already sold:**
```
Dr. Accounts Receivable                    $50,000
    Cr. Cost of Goods Sold                       $50,000
```

## Shipping and Handling Costs

### Accounting Policy Election

Entities have a policy choice for shipping and handling costs:

| Option | Treatment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cost of sales** | Include in COGS; reduces gross margin |
| **Operating expense** | Present separately; higher gross margin |

### Consistency Required

- Apply consistently to similar transactions
- Disclose policy in financial statement notes

### Example Disclosure

> *Shipping and handling costs associated with outbound freight are included in selling expenses and totaled $X for the year ended December 31, 20XX.*

---

## Idle Facility and Abnormal Costs

### Treatment of Abnormal Costs

| Cost Type | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Normal spoilage** | Include in cost of inventory/COGS |
| **Abnormal spoilage** | Expense as incurred (not in COGS) |
| **Idle facility costs** | Expense as incurred |
| **Excess capacity costs** | May be excluded from inventory |

### Journal Entry—Abnormal Spoilage

**Scenario:** Manufacturing defect causes $25,000 of unusable inventory.

```
Dr. Loss on Abnormal Spoilage            $25,000
    Cr. Inventory                               $25,000
```

## Purchase Price Allocations

### Multiple-Element Purchases

When acquiring multiple items in a single transaction:
- Allocate purchase price based on relative fair values
- Or use residual method if one item's FV is known

### Example

**Scenario:** Purchase equipment bundle for $100,000. Individual FVs: Equipment A ($70,000), Equipment B ($30,000), Supplies ($10,000).

**Total FV:** $110,000

| Item | FV | Allocation | Allocated Cost |
|------|-----|------------|----------------|
| Equipment A | $70,000 | 63.6% | $63,600 |
| Equipment B | $30,000 | 27.3% | $27,300 |
| Supplies | $10,000 | 9.1% | $9,100 |
| **Total** | **$110,000** | **100%** | **$100,000** |

## Disclosure Requirements

### Required Disclosures

1. **Accounting policy** for cost of sales classification
2. **Shipping and handling** presentation policy
3. **Vendor consideration** policy and amounts
4. **Significant judgments** affecting cost recognition

### Example Note Disclosure

> **Cost of Sales:** Cost of sales includes the cost of merchandise sold, inbound freight, warehousing costs, and vendor allowances. Shipping and handling costs billed to customers are included in net sales, while the costs of shipping and handling are included in cost of sales.

## Private Company Considerations

### Simplified Approaches

- Same guidance applies to private companies
- May have simpler cost accumulation systems
- Focus on consistent application of accounting policies

### Common Issues

1. Inconsistent classification between periods
2. Missing vendor rebate accruals
3. Improper capitalization of period costs
4. Inadequate overhead allocation

---

## Common Audit Issues

1. **Cutoff errors** — Costs recorded in wrong period
2. **Vendor rebates** — Not properly accrued or allocated
3. **Classification** — Period costs included in COGS
4. **Overhead allocation** — Improper methods or rates
5. **Abnormal costs** — Normal vs. abnormal not distinguished
6. **Shipping costs** — Inconsistent presentation
7. **Standard cost variances** — Not properly allocated
