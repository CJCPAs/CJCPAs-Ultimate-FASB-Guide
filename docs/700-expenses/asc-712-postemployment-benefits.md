# ASC 712: Compensation—Nonretirement Postemployment Benefits

> Accounting for benefits provided to former or inactive employees after employment but before retirement.

## Overview

ASC 712 addresses the accounting for postemployment benefits other than retirement benefits. These include severance pay, disability benefits, continuation of health coverage, and other benefits provided to former or inactive employees. This guidance bridges the gap between active employment benefits and retirement benefits.

**Core Principle:**
> "An employer shall accrue a liability for postemployment benefits if the obligation is attributable to employees' services already rendered, rights vest or accumulate, payment is probable, and the amount can be reasonably estimated."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Severance benefits
- Disability-related benefits
- Job training and counseling
- Continuation of health/life insurance coverage
- Benefits for inactive employees (layoff, leave of absence)

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Retirement benefits (ASC 715)
- Termination benefits under exit activities (ASC 420)
- Stock compensation (ASC 718)
- Active employee benefits (ASC 710)

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Postemployment Benefits** | Benefits provided after employment but before retirement |
| **Former Employees** | Those who have been terminated (involuntarily or voluntarily) |
| **Inactive Employees** | Those not currently working (disability, layoff) but not terminated |
| **Severance** | Benefits upon involuntary termination |
| **Disability Benefits** | Payments due to employee's inability to work |

---

## Types of Postemployment Benefits

### 1. Severance Benefits

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Contractual** | Specified in employment contract |
| **Policy-based** | Per company policy or practice |
| **Statutory** | Required by law (e.g., WARN Act) |
| **Negotiated** | Individual termination agreements |

### 2. Disability Benefits

| Duration | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Short-term** | Sick pay, short-term disability |
| **Long-term** | Long-term disability insurance |
| **Workers' compensation** | Work-related injury/illness |

### 3. Other Benefits

- Continuation of health insurance (COBRA, company-paid)
- Life insurance continuation
- Job training and outplacement
- Supplemental unemployment benefits

---

## Recognition Criteria

### Four Conditions for Accrual

Accrue if **ALL** conditions are met:

| Condition | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **1. Services rendered** | Obligation from services already performed |
| **2. Rights vest or accumulate** | Benefits vest or accumulate |
| **3. Payment probable** | Payment is probable |
| **4. Amount estimable** | Amount can be reasonably estimated |

### If All Conditions NOT Met

| Situation | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| 1-2 met, 3-4 not met | Disclose if material |
| Amount not estimable | Disclose inability to estimate |
| Remote likelihood | No accrual or disclosure |

---

## Measurement

### General Approach

Measure using:
- Best estimate if single amount
- Low end of range if no best estimate

### Components to Include

| Component | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Base severance** | Include |
| **Employer payroll taxes** | Include |
| **Benefit continuation** | Present value of future costs |
| **Outplacement services** | Estimated cost |

### Discounting

| Duration | Discount? |
|----------|-----------|
| **Payment within 12 months** | Generally no |
| **Payment beyond 12 months** | Yes—use appropriate rate |

## Journal Entries

### Accrual of Severance Benefits

**Policy-based severance (probable and estimable):**
```
Dr. Severance Expense                $250,000
    Cr. Accrued Severance                     $250,000
```

### Disability Benefits Accrual

**When employee becomes disabled:**
```
Dr. Disability Benefits Expense       $75,000
    Cr. Disability Benefits Liability         $75,000
(PV of expected future payments)
```

### Payment of Benefits

**Severance payment:**
```
Dr. Accrued Severance                 $25,000
    Cr. Cash                                   $25,000
```

**Disability payment:**
```
Dr. Disability Benefits Liability      $3,000
    Cr. Cash                                    $3,000
```

## Severance Benefits—Detailed Guidance

### When to Recognize

| Arrangement Type | Recognition Timing |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Ongoing benefit plan** | When probable and estimable |
| **One-time arrangement** | Follow ASC 420 |
| **Individual agreement** | When agreement signed |

### Severance Calculation Example

**Facts:**
- Employee terminated after 15 years
- Severance policy: 2 weeks per year of service
- Weekly salary: $2,000
- Employer payroll taxes: 7.65%

**Calculation:**
```
Base severance: 15 years × 2 weeks × $2,000 = $60,000
Payroll taxes: $60,000 × 7.65% = $4,590
Total accrual: $64,590
```

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Severance Expense                $64,590
    Cr. Accrued Severance                     $64,590
```

## Disability Benefits—Detailed Guidance

### Short-Term vs. Long-Term

| Type | Recognition |
|------|-------------|
| **Short-term (< 1 year)** | Accrue when disability occurs |
| **Long-term (> 1 year)** | PV of expected payments |

### Long-Term Disability Example

**Facts:**
- Employee disabled at age 50
- LTD benefit: $3,000/month until age 65
- Probability of remaining disabled: 80%
- Discount rate: 5%

**Calculation:**
```
Monthly benefit: $3,000
Months until age 65: 180 (15 years × 12)
PV of annuity factor (5%, 180 months): 118.5
Expected PV: $3,000 × 118.5 × 80% = $284,400
```

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. LTD Expense                      $284,400
    Cr. LTD Liability                        $284,400
```

## COBRA and Health Benefit Continuation

### Employer-Paid COBRA

When employer pays COBRA premiums for terminated employees:

**Monthly Accrual:**
```
Dr. COBRA Expense                     $2,500
    Cr. COBRA Liability                        $2,500
(Accrued for expected coverage period)
```

### Subsidized Health Coverage

If employer provides subsidized coverage to former employees:

```
Dr. Postemployment Health Expense    $15,000
    Cr. Postemployment Health Liability      $15,000
(PV of expected subsidy)
```

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Workforce Reduction

**Facts:**
- 50 employees laid off
- Severance policy: 1 week per year of service
- Average years of service: 8 years
- Average weekly pay: $1,000
- COBRA coverage: 3 months at $800/month per employee

**Severance Calculation:**
```
Base: 50 × 8 weeks × $1,000 = $400,000
Taxes (7.65%): $30,600
COBRA: 50 × 3 × $800 = $120,000
Total: $550,600
```

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Severance and Benefits Expense   $550,600
    Cr. Accrued Postemployment Benefits      $550,600
```

---

### Example 2: Disability Claim

**Facts:**
- Employee becomes permanently disabled
- Company self-insures disability
- Monthly benefit: $4,000
- Expected payment period: 10 years
- Discount rate: 4%

**Calculation:**
```
Annual benefit: $4,000 × 12 = $48,000
PV factor (4%, 10 years): 8.1109
PV of benefits: $48,000 × 8.1109 = $389,323
```

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Disability Expense               $389,323
    Cr. Disability Liability                 $389,323
```

**Annual Payment:**
```
Dr. Disability Liability              $48,000
    Cr. Cash                                   $48,000
```

---

### Example 3: Liability Rollforward

```
POSTEMPLOYMENT BENEFITS LIABILITY ROLLFORWARD
For the Year Ended December 31, 20X1

                          Severance   Disability   Other     Total
Beginning balance         $  200,000   $500,000   $ 50,000  $750,000
Expense accrued              400,000    150,000     25,000   575,000
Benefits paid               (350,000)   (80,000)   (30,000) (460,000)
Changes in estimates         (25,000)    20,000         -     (5,000)
                          ----------   --------   --------  --------
Ending balance            $  225,000   $590,000   $ 45,000  $860,000
```

## Interaction with ASC 420

### Key Distinctions

| Factor | ASC 712 | ASC 420 |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Arrangement type** | Ongoing benefit plan | One-time termination |
| **Trigger** | Individual circumstance | Exit activity |
| **Recognition** | When conditions met | When plan communicated |

### Decision Framework

```
Is this part of an exit/disposal activity?
         ↓
    Yes → ASC 420 (Exit Costs)
         ↓
    No
         ↓
Is there an ongoing benefit plan/policy?
         ↓
    Yes → ASC 712 (Postemployment)
         ↓
    No → Individual arrangement analysis
```

## Private Company Considerations

### Common Scenarios

| Scenario | Treatment |
|----------|-----------|
| **Owner retirement** | May trigger severance/buyout |
| **Key person disability** | May be self-insured |
| **Small layoffs** | Still analyze under ASC 712 |

### Documentation

- Maintain written policies
- Document estimates and assumptions
- Track individual arrangements
- Monitor disability claims

## Disclosure Requirements

### Required Disclosures

| Item | Disclosure |
|------|------------|
| **Description** | Nature of postemployment benefits |
| **Accounting policy** | Basis for recognition and measurement |
| **Amount** | Liability recorded |
| **Uncertainties** | Significant uncertainties |

### Example Disclosure

```
NOTE X: POSTEMPLOYMENT BENEFITS

The Company provides severance benefits to employees involuntarily
terminated under its severance policy. Benefits include base
severance based on years of service and continued health insurance
coverage for specified periods. The accrued severance liability
was $225,000 and $200,000 at December 31, 20X1 and 20X0.

The Company self-insures certain disability benefits for employees
who become permanently disabled. The liability for future benefit
payments was $590,000 and $500,000 at December 31, 20X1 and 20X0,
based on actuarial estimates using a 4% discount rate.

Total postemployment benefit expense was $575,000 and $480,000
for the years ended December 31, 20X1 and 20X0, respectively.
```
