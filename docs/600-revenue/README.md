## Key Standards

### ASC 606: Revenue from Contracts with Customers

📌 **Major Standard — The core revenue recognition framework**

See detailed guide: [ASC 606: Revenue Recognition](asc-606-revenue-recognition.md)

**Overview:**

ASC 606 provides a single, comprehensive revenue recognition model for recognizing revenue from contracts with customers across all industries.

**Core Principle:**
> "Recognize revenue to depict the transfer of promised goods or services to customers in an amount that reflects the consideration to which the entity expects to be entitled in exchange for those goods or services."

**The Five-Step Model:**

| Step | Description | Key Questions |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **Step 1** | Identify the contract | Is there a valid contract with commercial substance? |
| **Step 2** | Identify performance obligations | What promises did we make to the customer? |
| **Step 3** | Determine transaction price | How much will we receive? |
| **Step 4** | Allocate transaction price | How do we split price among obligations? |
| **Step 5** | Recognize revenue | When is each obligation satisfied? |

---

## The Five-Step Model in Detail

### Step 1: Identify the Contract

**Contract Criteria (all must be met):**
1. ✅ Approval and commitment by parties
2. ✅ Rights of parties identified
3. ✅ Payment terms identified
4. ✅ Commercial substance
5. ✅ Probable collection of consideration

**Plain English:** You have a real deal where both sides know what they're getting, and you'll likely get paid.

### Step 2: Identify Performance Obligations

**A performance obligation is:**
- A promise to transfer a distinct good or service
- A series of distinct goods/services that are substantially the same

**Distinct if (both required):**
1. **Capable of being distinct** — Customer can benefit from good/service on its own
2. **Distinct in contract** — Promise is separately identifiable from other promises

**Examples:**

| Arrangement | Performance Obligations |
|-------------|------------------------|
| Software + 1-year support | 2 obligations (software license, support) |
| Consulting services | 1 obligation (series of similar services) |
| Equipment + installation | Depends—is installation highly integrated? |

### Step 3: Determine Transaction Price

**Considerations:**
- Fixed amounts
- Variable consideration (estimate using expected value or most likely amount)
- Significant financing component (time value of money)
- Noncash consideration (fair value)
- Consideration payable to customer (reduce transaction price)

**Variable Consideration Constraint:**
Include variable amounts only to extent it's probable a significant reversal won't occur.

### Step 4: Allocate Transaction Price

**Allocation based on standalone selling prices (SSP):**
1. **Observable SSP** — Price when sold separately
2. **Estimated SSP** — If not observable:
   - Adjusted market assessment approach
   - Expected cost plus margin approach
   - Residual approach (limited circumstances)

### Step 5: Recognize Revenue

**Point in Time Recognition:**
When control transfers (indicators below)

**Over Time Recognition (any one criterion):**
1. Customer receives and consumes benefits simultaneously
2. Entity's performance creates/enhances customer-controlled asset
3. No alternative use + right to payment for performance to date

**Control Transfer Indicators (point in time):**
- Present right to payment
- Customer has legal title
- Physical possession transferred
- Significant risks/rewards transferred
- Customer acceptance

## Special Topics

### Contract Modifications

| Type | Treatment |
|------|-----------|
| Separate contract | Account prospectively |
| Not separate—distinct goods/services | Prospective; allocate remaining consideration |
| Not separate—not distinct | Cumulative catch-up adjustment |

### Principal vs. Agent

| Role | Revenue Recognition |
|------|---------------------|
| **Principal** (controls good/service) | Gross revenue |
| **Agent** (arranges for another party) | Net revenue (commission) |

### Bill-and-Hold Arrangements

Revenue recognized before delivery if:
1. Reason for arrangement is substantive
2. Product separately identified as customer's
3. Product ready to transfer
4. No ability to use product or direct to another customer

### Contract Costs

| Cost Type | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Costs to obtain contract | Capitalize if incremental and recoverable |
| Costs to fulfill contract | Capitalize if directly related, generate resources, recoverable |
| Practical expedient | Expense if amortization ≤ 1 year |

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Software with Support

**Facts:** Company sells software license ($800) and 2-year support ($400) for $1,000 total.

**SSP Analysis:**
- Software SSP: $800
- Support SSP: $200/year

**Allocation:**
- Total SSP: $1,200
- Software: $1,000 × ($800/$1,200) = $667
- Support: $1,000 × ($400/$1,200) = $333

**Revenue Recognition:**
```
At delivery of software:
Dr. Accounts Receivable          $1,000
    Cr. Revenue—Software                 $667
    Cr. Deferred Revenue—Support         $333

Monthly over 2 years:
Dr. Deferred Revenue             $13.88
    Cr. Revenue—Support                  $13.88
```

### Example 2: Construction Contract (Over Time)

**Facts:** 3-year construction project. Total price: $3M. Estimated costs: $2.4M.

**Year 1:** Costs incurred $800K (33.3% complete)

**Revenue Recognition:**
```
Dr. Accounts Receivable          $1,000,000
    Cr. Revenue                          $1,000,000

Dr. Cost of Construction         $800,000
    Cr. Inventory/Cash/Payables          $800,000
```

## Private Company Considerations

### Practical Expedients Available

| Expedient | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Significant financing | Ignore if payment expected within 1 year |
| Costs to obtain | Expense if amortization ≤ 1 year |
| Shipping/handling | Can treat as fulfillment cost, not separate obligation |
| Taxes collected | Exclude from transaction price |

### Common Private Company Scenarios

1. **Long-term contracts** — Measure progress appropriately
2. **Warranty obligations** — Distinguish assurance-type from service-type
3. **Customer loyalty programs** — Account for material rights

## Disclosure Requirements

### Annual Disclosures

1. **Disaggregation of revenue** — By type, timing, geography
2. **Contract balances** — Receivables, contract assets, contract liabilities
3. **Performance obligations** — Nature, timing, significant terms
4. **Significant judgments** — Timing of satisfaction, transaction price allocation
5. **Costs to obtain/fulfill** — Closing balances, amortization, impairments

### Interim Disclosures

Disaggregation of revenue required; other disclosures encouraged but not required.

---

## Common Audit Issues

1. **Performance Obligation Identification** — Over- or under-bundling
2. **Variable Consideration** — Inadequate constraint analysis
3. **Over Time vs. Point in Time** — Incorrect timing of recognition
4. **Principal vs. Agent** — Gross vs. net presentation errors
5. **Contract Modifications** — Improper treatment
