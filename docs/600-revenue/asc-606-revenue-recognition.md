# ASC 606: Revenue from Contracts with Customers

> The comprehensive revenue recognition standard for virtually all industries.

## Overview

ASC 606 establishes a single, principles-based framework for recognizing revenue from contracts with customers. It replaced most industry-specific guidance and applies to all entities that enter into contracts to transfer goods or services to customers.

**Core Principle:**
> "An entity shall recognize revenue to depict the transfer of promised goods or services to customers in an amount that reflects the consideration to which the entity expects to be entitled in exchange for those goods or services."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Contracts with customers for transfer of goods or services
- Most sales of nonfinancial assets (if not a customer contract)

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Lease contracts (ASC 842)
- Insurance contracts (ASC 944)
- Financial instruments (ASC 310, 320, 470, etc.)
- Guarantees (ASC 460)
- Nonmonetary exchanges between entities in the same line of business

## The Five-Step Model

### Step 1: Identify the Contract with a Customer

**Contract Criteria (ALL must be met):**

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| 1. Approval | Both parties approved the contract and are committed to their obligations |
| 2. Rights | Each party's rights are identifiable |
| 3. Payment terms | Payment terms are identifiable |
| 4. Commercial substance | The contract has commercial substance (changes risk, timing, or amount of cash flows) |
| 5. Collectibility | Collection of consideration is probable |

**Plain English:** You have a real deal if both sides agreed to it, know what they're getting, know when payment is due, the deal changes your business in some real way, and you're likely to actually get paid.

**Contract Combinations:**
Combine contracts if negotiated as a package with a single commercial objective, or consideration in one depends on the other, or goods/services are a single performance obligation.

**Contract Modifications:**
- If modification adds distinct goods/services at standalone prices → Separate contract
- If modification adds distinct goods/services not at standalone prices → Prospective adjustment
- If modification does not add distinct goods/services → Cumulative catch-up

---

### Step 2: Identify the Performance Obligations

**Definition:** A promise to transfer a distinct good or service (or bundle of goods/services).

**Distinct if BOTH are met:**

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| Capable of being distinct | Can the customer benefit from the good/service on its own (or with readily available resources)? |
| Distinct within the context | Is the promise separately identifiable from other promises in the contract? |

**Indicators that promises are NOT separately identifiable:**
- Significant integration service
- Goods/services significantly modify or customize each other
- Highly interdependent or interrelated

**Series Guidance:**
A series of distinct goods/services that are substantially the same with the same pattern of transfer is a single performance obligation.

### Common Examples

| Arrangement | Performance Obligations | Rationale |
|-------------|------------------------|-----------|
| Smartphone + 2-year service | 2 (phone, service) | Each is distinct; customer can use phone with other carriers |
| Software + implementation (highly customized) | 1 (combined) | Significant customization means not separately identifiable |
| Equipment + installation (routine) | 2 (equipment, installation) | Customer could install themselves or hire another party |
| Subscription service | 1 (series) | Each month is substantially the same |

---

### Step 3: Determine the Transaction Price

**Transaction Price:** The amount of consideration the entity expects to be entitled to.

**Consider:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Variable consideration** | Discounts, rebates, refunds, price concessions, incentives, bonuses, penalties |
| **Constraining variable consideration** | Include only to extent significant reversal is not probable |
| **Significant financing component** | Adjust for time value of money if > 1 year |
| **Noncash consideration** | Measure at fair value |
| **Consideration payable to customer** | Reduce transaction price (or apply as expense if for distinct goods/services) |

**Methods for Estimating Variable Consideration:**

| Method | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|
| **Expected value** | Large number of similar contracts |
| **Most likely amount** | Only two possible outcomes (e.g., achieve bonus or don't) |

**Variable Consideration Constraint:**
Include in transaction price only to extent it's probable that a significant reversal won't occur when uncertainty is resolved.

**Factors increasing likelihood of reversal:**
- Susceptible to factors outside entity's influence
- Long period before uncertainty resolved
- Limited experience with similar contracts
- Practice of offering broad range of price concessions
- Large number and broad range of possible outcomes

---

### Step 4: Allocate the Transaction Price

**Allocation Basis:** Relative standalone selling prices (SSP)

**Determining SSP:**

| Method | Description | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Observable price** | Price when sold separately | Best evidence—use when available |
| **Adjusted market assessment** | What the market would pay | When observable price unavailable |
| **Expected cost plus margin** | Expected costs plus appropriate margin | When observable price unavailable |
| **Residual approach** | Transaction price less other SSPs | Only when SSP highly variable or uncertain |

**Allocation of Discounts:**
- Allocate proportionally to all performance obligations
- Unless: Allocate entirely to one or more (if criteria met)

**Allocation of Variable Consideration:**
- Generally allocate to all performance obligations
- May allocate to specific obligation(s) if directly related

---

### Step 5: Recognize Revenue When (or As) Performance Obligation Is Satisfied

**Control Transfers:**
Revenue is recognized when control of promised good/service transfers to customer.

**Over Time Recognition (if ANY ONE criterion met):**

| Criterion | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **1. Simultaneous receipt and consumption** | Customer receives and consumes benefits as entity performs | Cleaning services, routine recurring services |
| **2. Creates/enhances customer-controlled asset** | Entity creates or enhances asset that customer controls | Construction on customer's property |
| **3. No alternative use + right to payment** | Asset has no alternative use to entity AND entity has enforceable right to payment for performance to date | Custom manufacturing with termination provisions |

**Point in Time Recognition:**
If none of the over-time criteria are met, recognize revenue at the point in time when control transfers.

**Indicators of Control Transfer (point in time):**
- Present right to payment
- Customer has legal title
- Physical possession transferred
- Significant risks and rewards of ownership transferred
- Customer acceptance

**Measuring Progress (over time):**

| Method | How It Works |
|--------|--------------|
| **Output methods** | Direct measurement of value transferred (units delivered, milestones, surveys, etc.) |
| **Input methods** | Entity's efforts or inputs (costs incurred, labor hours, machine hours, etc.) |

Choose method that best depicts entity's performance.

## Special Topics

### Principal vs. Agent

**Key Question:** Does the entity control the good/service before it's transferred to customer?

| Role | Revenue Recognition | Indicators |
|------|---------------------|------------|
| **Principal** | Gross (full transaction price) | Controls goods/services before transfer |
| **Agent** | Net (commission only) | Arranges for another party to provide goods/services |

**Indicators of Control (Principal):**
- Primary responsibility for fulfillment
- Inventory risk (before transfer, after return, during shipping)
- Discretion in establishing prices

---

### Bill-and-Hold Arrangements

Revenue recognized before physical delivery if ALL met:
1. Substantive reason for arrangement (customer's request)
2. Product separately identified as belonging to customer
3. Product currently ready for physical transfer
4. Entity cannot use product or direct it to another customer

---

### Licensing

**Types of Licenses:**

| Type | When to Recognize |
|------|-------------------|
| **Right to access** (dynamic IP) | Over time | Entity continues to be involved with IP |
| **Right to use** (static IP) | Point in time | Customer can use IP as it exists at grant date |

**Dynamic IP (over time) if ALL met:**
1. Entity will undertake activities that significantly affect the IP
2. Customer is exposed to effects of those activities
3. Activities do not transfer a good/service to customer as they occur

---

### Contract Costs

**Costs to Obtain a Contract:**
- Capitalize if incremental (would not have been incurred without contract)
- Capitalize only if expected to be recovered
- **Practical expedient:** Expense if amortization period ≤ 1 year

**Costs to Fulfill a Contract:**
Capitalize if:
1. Directly relate to a contract (or anticipated contract)
2. Generate or enhance resources to satisfy future performance obligations
3. Expected to be recovered

## Disclosure Requirements

### Qualitative Disclosures

1. Contracts with customers (nature, significant terms)
2. Performance obligations (when satisfied, significant payment terms)
3. Significant judgments (timing, transaction price allocation)

### Quantitative Disclosures

1. **Disaggregation of revenue** — By category that depicts how economic factors affect nature, amount, timing, and uncertainty
2. **Contract balances** — Opening/closing receivables, contract assets, contract liabilities
3. **Performance obligations** — Aggregate transaction price allocated to remaining obligations
4. **Significant changes** — In contract balances, remaining performance obligations

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Software License with Support

**Facts:**
- Software license: $10,000 (SSP: $12,000)
- 2-year support: $4,000 (SSP: $4,000)
- Total contract price: $14,000

**Step 2:** Two performance obligations (software is distinct from support)

**Step 4:** Allocate based on relative SSP
- Total SSP: $16,000
- Software: $14,000 × ($12,000/$16,000) = $10,500
- Support: $14,000 × ($4,000/$16,000) = $3,500

**Step 5:**
- Software: Point in time (when delivered)
- Support: Over time (ratably over 2 years)

**Journal Entries:**

*At delivery of software:*
```
Dr. Accounts Receivable          $14,000
    Cr. Revenue—Software                 $10,500
    Cr. Deferred Revenue—Support         $3,500
```

*Monthly over 2 years (support):*
```
Dr. Deferred Revenue             $145.83
    Cr. Revenue—Support                  $145.83
```

---

### Example 2: Construction Contract

**Facts:**
- 18-month construction project
- Fixed price: $5,000,000
- Estimated total costs: $4,000,000
- Costs incurred to date: $1,600,000

**Analysis:**
- Satisfies over-time criterion (no alternative use + right to payment)
- Use cost-to-cost input method

**Progress:** $1,600,000 / $4,000,000 = 40%

**Revenue to recognize:** $5,000,000 × 40% = $2,000,000

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Contract Asset               $400,000
Dr. Cash/AR                      $1,600,000
    Cr. Revenue                          $2,000,000

Dr. Contract Costs               $1,600,000
    Cr. Cash/Payables                    $1,600,000
```

---

### Example 3: Variable Consideration

**Facts:**
- Equipment sale: $100,000 base price
- Volume rebate: $10,000 if customer purchases 1,000+ units during year
- Based on history, 70% probability of achieving volume threshold

**Analysis:**
Use expected value method: $10,000 × 70% = $7,000 estimated rebate

**Transaction Price:** $100,000 - $7,000 = $93,000

**Constraint Assessment:** Is it probable that significant reversal won't occur? Consider factors and document conclusion.

## Private Company Considerations

### Practical Expedients Available to All Entities

| Expedient | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Significant financing component | Ignore if payment expected within 1 year |
| Costs to obtain contract | Expense if amortization ≤ 1 year |
| Shipping and handling | Can treat as fulfillment cost (not separate obligation) after control transfers |
| Taxes collected | Can exclude from transaction price |
| Portfolio approach | Apply to portfolio of similar contracts if reasonable |

### Common Private Company Scenarios

1. **Long-term contracts** — Ensure proper measure of progress
2. **Multiple-element arrangements** — Identify all performance obligations
3. **Extended warranties** — Distinguish service-type from assurance-type
4. **Customer options** — Evaluate material rights

---

## Common Implementation Issues

### 1. Identifying Performance Obligations
- Over-bundling (treating distinct items as combined)
- Under-bundling (separating when items are highly interdependent)

### 2. Variable Consideration
- Not applying the constraint properly
- Using wrong estimation method

### 3. Principal vs. Agent
- Defaulting to historical treatment without analysis
- Misapplying indicators

### 4. Over Time vs. Point in Time
- Not evaluating all three over-time criteria
- Misinterpreting "no alternative use"

### 5. Disclosures
- Insufficient disaggregation
- Missing remaining performance obligation disclosures
