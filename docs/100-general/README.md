## ASC 105: Generally Accepted Accounting Principles

### Overview

ASC 105 establishes the hierarchy of generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) for nongovernmental entities in the United States.

### Official Codification Language

> "The FASB Accounting Standards Codification® is the source of authoritative generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) recognized by the FASB to be applied by nongovernmental entities." — ASC 105-10-05-1

### Plain English Interpretation

**What this means for you:**

The Codification (ASC) is your single, authoritative source for GAAP. Everything else (textbooks, articles, firm guidance) is non-authoritative and should only be used for additional context.

**The GAAP Hierarchy:**
1. **Authoritative GAAP** — The ASC itself
2. **Non-authoritative guidance** — Everything else (can be helpful but not required)

### Key Concepts

#### What Is Authoritative?

| Authoritative | Non-Authoritative |
|---------------|-------------------|
| ASC Topics | AICPA Technical Q&As |
| SEC Staff guidance (for public companies) | Big Four publications |
| | Academic articles |
| | Industry publications |

#### When No Specific Guidance Exists

If the Codification doesn't address your transaction, consider (in order):
1. Guidance for similar transactions in the ASC
2. Non-authoritative sources (consistent with ASC concepts)

### Practical Application

**Example: New Type of Transaction**

A company enters into a unique arrangement not specifically addressed in the ASC.

**Steps:**
1. Search the ASC for similar transaction types
2. Apply analogous guidance by analogy
3. Document the basis for your accounting treatment
4. Consider consulting with auditors

### Disclosure Requirements

No specific disclosures required by ASC 105, but companies should describe significant accounting policies under ASC 235.

### Private Company Considerations

Private companies follow the same GAAP hierarchy but may apply:
- Private Company Council (PCC) alternatives
- Practical expedients available to non-public business entities
