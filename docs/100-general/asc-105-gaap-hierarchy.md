# ASC 105: Generally Accepted Accounting Principles

> The foundation of all financial reporting—understanding the GAAP hierarchy and authoritative guidance.

## Overview

ASC 105 establishes the framework for identifying the sources of authoritative generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) for nongovernmental entities. It defines the GAAP hierarchy and clarifies what constitutes authoritative guidance that must be followed versus nonauthoritative guidance that may be consulted.

**Core Principle:**
> "The FASB Accounting Standards Codification® is the source of authoritative generally accepted accounting principles recognized by the FASB to be applied by nongovernmental entities."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- All nongovernmental entities preparing financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP
- Auditors evaluating GAAP compliance
- Any entity applying authoritative accounting literature

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Governmental entities (follow GASB)
- Federal entities (follow FASAB)
- Tax-basis or other comprehensive bases of accounting (OCBOA)
- Financial statements prepared under IFRS

---

## The GAAP Hierarchy

### Pre-Codification (Historical Context)

Prior to the Codification (before 2009), GAAP consisted of multiple levels:

| Level | Sources |
|-------|---------|
| Level A | FASB Statements, Interpretations, APB Opinions, ARBs |
| Level B | FASB Technical Bulletins, AICPA Industry Guides |
| Level C | AICPA Practice Bulletins, Consensus positions of EITF |
| Level D | AICPA Accounting Interpretations, Implementation Guides |

### Post-Codification (Current)

**The Codification is now the single source of authoritative GAAP:**

| Category | Description | Status |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| **FASB ASC** | All codified content | **Authoritative** |
| **SEC Content** | SEC rules, interpretive releases, SAB, EITF D-Topics | Authoritative for SEC registrants |
| **All Other** | Non-codified guidance, textbooks, articles | **Nonauthoritative** |

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)** | The rules, conventions, and procedures that define accepted accounting practice at a particular time |
| **Authoritative Guidance** | Accounting guidance that is included in the Codification |
| **Nonauthoritative Guidance** | Sources of accounting guidance not included in the Codification (may be helpful but not required) |
| **Codification** | The single source of authoritative nongovernmental U.S. GAAP (ASC) |
| **ASU (Accounting Standards Update)** | The mechanism by which the FASB communicates changes to the Codification |

## The FASB Codification Structure

### Organization

The Codification is organized into the following structure:

```
Area → Topic → Subtopic → Section → Paragraph
```

### Areas (Broadest Classification)

| Area | Number Range | Description |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| **General Principles** | 100-199 | General concepts and principles |
| **Presentation** | 200-299 | Financial statement presentation |
| **Assets** | 300-399 | Asset recognition and measurement |
| **Liabilities** | 400-499 | Liability recognition and measurement |
| **Equity** | 500-599 | Equity transactions |
| **Revenue** | 600-699 | Revenue recognition |
| **Expenses** | 700-799 | Expense recognition |
| **Broad Transactions** | 800-899 | Transactions affecting multiple areas |
| **Industry** | 900-999 | Industry-specific guidance |

### Section Types

| Section | Number | Content |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Status | 00 | Links to transition guidance |
| Objectives | 05 | Purpose and overview |
| Scope and Scope Exceptions | 15 | What's covered |
| Definitions | 20 | Key terms |
| Recognition | 25 | When to record |
| Initial Measurement | 30 | How to measure initially |
| Subsequent Measurement | 35 | Ongoing measurement |
| Derecognition | 40 | When to remove |
| Other Presentation Matters | 45 | Display requirements |
| Disclosure | 50 | Required disclosures |
| Implementation Guidance | 55 | Examples and illustrations |
| Relationships | 60 | Links to other topics |

## Applying GAAP

### When Codification Is Silent

If the Codification doesn't address a transaction or event, consider:

1. **Guidance for similar transactions** within the Codification
2. **Nonauthoritative guidance** from other sources

### Hierarchy of Nonauthoritative Sources

While not required, these sources may be helpful:

| Source Type | Examples |
|-------------|----------|
| **FASB Resources** | Concepts Statements, pre-Codification standards, ASU Basis for Conclusions |
| **AICPA Resources** | Technical Practice Aids, Audit and Accounting Guides |
| **Other Sources** | SEC Observer Comments, IFRS (for non-SEC filers), industry practices |
| **Academic/Professional** | Textbooks, journal articles, expert opinions |

**Important:** Nonauthoritative guidance cannot conflict with the Codification.

## SEC Registrant Considerations

### Additional Authoritative Content

For SEC registrants, the following are also authoritative:

| Source | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Regulation S-X** | Form and content of financial statements |
| **Regulation S-K** | Non-financial statement disclosures |
| **SEC Rules and Releases** | Formal SEC pronouncements |
| **Staff Accounting Bulletins (SAB)** | SEC staff interpretations |
| **SEC Observer Comments** | EITF meeting comments |
| **SEC Staff Announcements** | AICPA conference speeches |

### SEC Content in Codification

SEC content is included in the Codification in two ways:
- **"S" Sections** (e.g., 105-10-S99): SEC-specific requirements
- **Shaded Text**: SEC guidance within general sections

## Accounting Standards Updates (ASUs)

### What ASUs Are

ASUs are the mechanism for updating the Codification. They:
- Communicate changes to the Codification
- Provide background and basis for conclusions
- Include transition guidance and effective dates

### What ASUs Are NOT

- ASUs themselves are **not** authoritative
- Only the resulting Codification amendments are authoritative
- ASUs should not be cited as the source of GAAP (cite the Codification)

### Monitoring Updates

| Resource | Content |
|----------|---------|
| **FASB Website** | All issued ASUs and exposure drafts |
| **Codification Notice to Constituents** | Pending content awaiting codification |
| **FASB Technical Agenda** | Upcoming projects and expected changes |

## GAAP vs. Other Frameworks

### Comparison of Accounting Frameworks

| Framework | Standard Setter | Applies To |
|-----------|-----------------|------------|
| **U.S. GAAP** | FASB | U.S. nongovernmental entities |
| **IFRS** | IASB | International (and some U.S. foreign filers) |
| **GASB Standards** | GASB | State and local governments |
| **FASAB Standards** | FASAB | Federal government entities |
| **OCBOA** | Various | Tax-basis, cash-basis, etc. |

### GAAP Departures

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **GAAP Departure** | Financial statements that deviate from GAAP |
| **Qualified Opinion** | Auditor opinion when GAAP departure is material |
| **Adverse Opinion** | Auditor opinion when GAAP departure is pervasive |

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Researching a Transaction

**Scenario:** Entity needs guidance on accounting for a software arrangement.

**Research Process:**
1. Identify relevant Codification topic (ASC 606 for revenue, ASC 985 for software)
2. Review scope to confirm applicability
3. Apply specific guidance in recognition, measurement, disclosure sections
4. Document conclusions with specific paragraph citations

**Citation Format:**
```
Per ASC 606-10-25-1, an entity shall recognize revenue when (or as)
the entity satisfies a performance obligation...
```

---

### Example 2: No Direct Guidance

**Scenario:** Entity enters a novel transaction not specifically addressed in the Codification.

**Approach:**
1. Search Codification for analogous transactions
2. Consider similar guidance by analogy
3. Review nonauthoritative sources:
   - FASB Concepts Statements
   - Industry practices
   - IFRS treatment
4. Apply professional judgment
5. **Document rationale thoroughly**

**Journal Entry Documentation:**
```
Memo: Based on analysis of ASC XXX-XX-XX-X by analogy to [similar
transaction], and consistent with Concepts Statement No. 5 regarding
recognition criteria, management has determined that [treatment] is
appropriate. This conclusion is also consistent with [nonauthoritative
source] treatment of similar arrangements.
```

---

### Example 3: Conflict Between Sources

**Scenario:** A textbook suggests one treatment, but the Codification indicates another.

**Resolution:**
- **Always follow the Codification**
- Nonauthoritative sources cannot override authoritative GAAP
- Document reliance on Codification citation

## Private Company Considerations

### Private Company Council (PCC)

The PCC was established to identify and address issues specific to private companies:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **PCC Alternatives** | Simplified accounting available only to private companies |
| **Election** | Usually entity-wide and irrevocable |
| **Examples** | Goodwill amortization, VIE exemption, hedge accounting simplification |

### Private Company Decision-Making Framework

1. **Evaluate** if PCC alternatives are available for the transaction
2. **Consider** cost-benefit of alternative vs. standard GAAP
3. **Document** election and consistent application
4. **Disclose** use of private company alternatives

### Finding PCC Alternatives

PCC alternatives are identified throughout the Codification:
- Look for "private company" or "PCC" references
- Section 65 often contains private company alternatives
- Master Glossary defines "private company" and "public business entity"

## Common Issues and Misconceptions

### Issue 1: Citing Pre-Codification Standards

**Wrong:** "Per FAS 123(R), stock compensation..."
**Right:** "Per ASC 718-10-25-1, stock compensation..."

### Issue 2: Relying on ASU Text

**Wrong:** "ASU 2016-02 requires..."
**Right:** "ASC 842-10-25-1 requires..."

### Issue 3: Using Nonauthoritative Over Authoritative

**Wrong:** Following Big Four firm guidance that conflicts with Codification
**Right:** Following Codification; using firm guidance only when Codification is silent

### Issue 4: Missing SEC Content

**Wrong:** SEC registrant ignores SAB guidance
**Right:** SEC registrant applies both Codification and applicable SEC content

## Research Tips

### Effective Codification Research

1. **Use the Master Glossary** — Understand defined terms
2. **Check Scope Sections** — Confirm the topic applies to your situation
3. **Review Implementation Guidance** — Section 55 often has helpful examples
4. **Check Pending Content** — "Notice to Constituents" shows upcoming changes
5. **Cross-Reference Related Topics** — Use Section 60 relationships

### Citation Best Practices

| Element | Format |
|---------|--------|
| **Full Citation** | ASC 606-10-25-1 |
| **Topic** | 606 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers) |
| **Subtopic** | 10 (Overall) |
| **Section** | 25 (Recognition) |
| **Paragraph** | 1 |

## Disclosure Requirements

### General GAAP Disclosures

Entities should disclose:
- That financial statements are prepared in conformity with U.S. GAAP
- Significant accounting policies (see ASC 235)
- Any departures from GAAP (rare—would typically be qualified/adverse audit opinion)

### Private Company Alternative Disclosures

When using PCC alternatives, disclose:
- The alternative being applied
- Nature of the simplified approach
- Consistent application across periods
