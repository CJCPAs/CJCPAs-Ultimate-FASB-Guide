# ASC 820: Fair Value Measurement

> The framework for measuring fair value and required disclosures about fair value measurements.

## Overview

ASC 820 establishes a single definition of fair value, sets out a framework for measuring fair value, and requires disclosures about fair value measurements. It applies when other ASC topics require or permit fair value measurements.

**Core Definition:**
> Fair value is the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date.

---

## Scope

### Applies When Other GAAP Requires or Permits Fair Value

ASC 820 does NOT require fair value measurements—it provides guidance on HOW to measure fair value when required by other standards.

**Common Applications:**
- Business combinations (ASC 805)
- Impairment testing
- Financial instruments (ASC 320, 321, 815, 825)
- Stock compensation (ASC 718)
- Certain nonfinancial assets/liabilities

### Does NOT Apply To:

- Share-based payment transactions (ASC 718) — Uses ASC 820 concepts but has specific guidance
- Inventory at NRV (ASC 330)
- Leases (ASC 842)
- Asset retirement obligations initial measurement (ASC 410)
- Certain employee benefit obligations (ASC 715)

---

## Key Concepts

### Exit Price

Fair value is an **exit price**—the price to sell an asset or transfer a liability (not an entry price or in-use value).

### Market Participants

Hypothetical buyers and sellers in the **principal market** (or most advantageous market):
- Independent of each other
- Knowledgeable
- Able to transact
- Willing to transact

### Principal vs. Most Advantageous Market

| Market | Description | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Principal market** | Market with greatest volume/activity | Use if identifiable |
| **Most advantageous market** | Market that maximizes amount received/minimizes amount paid | Use if no principal market |

---

## Fair Value Hierarchy

### Three Levels of Inputs

| Level | Description | Examples |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Level 1** | Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets/liabilities | NYSE stock price, Treasury yields |
| **Level 2** | Observable inputs other than Level 1 | Quoted prices for similar assets, interest rate swaps, dealer quotes |
| **Level 3** | Unobservable inputs | DCF with entity-developed assumptions, private company valuations |

### Hierarchy Requirements

- Prioritize Level 1 inputs
- Minimize use of unobservable inputs
- Maximize use of observable inputs
- Level is determined by **lowest level input** that is significant to entire measurement

### Active vs. Inactive Markets

| Active Market | Inactive Market |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Frequent transactions | Few transactions |
| Readily available prices | Prices not current or vary substantially |
| Level 1 inputs | May need adjustments (Level 2 or 3) |

---

## Valuation Approaches

### Three Approaches

| Approach | Description | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Market approach** | Uses prices and other information from market transactions | When comparable market data available |
| **Income approach** | Converts future amounts to single present value | For income-generating assets |
| **Cost approach** | Reflects current replacement cost | When replacement cost is relevant |

### Common Valuation Techniques

**Market Approach:**
- Comparable company analysis
- Precedent transactions
- Market multiples

**Income Approach:**
- Discounted cash flow (DCF)
- Option pricing models
- Multi-period excess earnings method
- Relief from royalty method

**Cost Approach:**
- Replacement cost
- Reproduction cost

---

## Fair Value of Liabilities

### Measurement Principles

Fair value of a liability reflects:
- **Nonperformance risk** (including entity's own credit risk)
- Transfer to a market participant who would assume the obligation

### Restrictions on Transfer

If a liability is restricted from transfer, do not adjust fair value for the restriction (assume market participant would be similarly restricted).

### Own Credit Risk

| Item | Consideration |
|------|---------------|
| Credit standing | Entity's creditworthiness at measurement date |
| Collateral/guarantees | Effect on credit risk |
| Market perception | How market views entity's credit |

---

## Nonfinancial Assets

### Highest and Best Use

Fair value of nonfinancial assets considers the **highest and best use** by market participants.

**Highest and best use is:**
- Physically possible
- Legally permissible
- Financially feasible

**Can be:**
- In-use (generates value in combination with other assets)
- In-exchange (generates value on standalone basis)

### Defensive Assets

Assets acquired to prevent competitors from using them are still measured at highest and best use from market participant perspective (not entity's defensive intent).

---

## Day 1 Gains/Losses

### When Transaction Price ≠ Fair Value

| Scenario | Treatment |
|----------|-----------|
| Fair value from Level 1 input | Recognize gain/loss immediately |
| Fair value from valuation technique with Level 2/3 inputs | Defer difference unless other evidence supports fair value |

---

## Fair Value Option (ASC 825)

### Election

Entities may elect to measure certain financial assets and liabilities at fair value.

**Available for:**
- Most financial instruments
- Firm commitments (with certain characteristics)
- Written loan commitments
- Insurance contract obligations (under certain conditions)

**NOT available for:**
- Employee benefit obligations
- Lease classification/measurement
- Financial instruments classified in equity
- Consolidation decisions

### Once Elected

- Irrevocable
- Changes in fair value recognized in earnings
- Specific disclosures required

## Disclosure Requirements

### For All Fair Value Measurements

| Level | Required Disclosures |
|-------|---------------------|
| **All levels** | Fair value measurements by level; transfers between levels |
| **Level 2** | Description of valuation techniques and inputs |
| **Level 3** | Quantitative information about unobservable inputs; reconciliation (roll-forward); description of valuation process; sensitivity analysis |

### Recurring vs. Nonrecurring

| Type | Example | Disclosure |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Recurring** | Trading securities, derivatives | Every reporting period |
| **Nonrecurring** | Impaired assets | In period measured |

### Specific Disclosures

1. **Fair value hierarchy table** — Assets/liabilities by level
2. **Transfers** — Between Level 1, 2, and 3
3. **Valuation techniques** — Methods used and changes
4. **Significant inputs** — Observable and unobservable
5. **Level 3 reconciliation** — Opening balance, gains/losses, purchases, sales, transfers
6. **Sensitivity** — Effect of reasonably possible alternative assumptions (Level 3)

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Level 1 — Traded Security

**Facts:**
- Company holds 10,000 shares of Apple Inc. (AAPL)
- NYSE closing price at measurement date: $175

**Fair Value:**
10,000 × $175 = $1,750,000

**Level:** 1 (quoted price in active market for identical asset)

---

### Example 2: Level 2 — Interest Rate Swap

**Facts:**
- Company has interest rate swap (receive fixed, pay variable)
- Notional: $10,000,000
- Fixed rate: 4%
- Variable: SOFR + 50 bps
- Remaining term: 3 years

**Valuation:**
Present value of fixed cash flows minus present value of variable cash flows, using observable swap curves.

**Level:** 2 (observable inputs—SOFR curve, credit spreads)

---

### Example 3: Level 3 — Private Company Investment

**Facts:**
- Company holds 20% of private company
- No recent transactions
- Using DCF model

**Assumptions (unobservable):**
- Revenue growth: 10%
- EBITDA margin: 25%
- Discount rate: 15%
- Terminal multiple: 6x EBITDA

**Level:** 3 (significant unobservable inputs)

**Required Disclosures:**
- Reconciliation of beginning to ending balance
- Quantitative information about each unobservable input
- Sensitivity analysis for significant changes in assumptions

---

### Example 4: Real Estate (Cost Approach)

**Facts:**
- Specialized manufacturing facility
- Limited market transactions
- Appraiser uses cost approach

**Approach:**
Replacement cost new minus depreciation (physical, functional, economic)

**Level:** Typically Level 3 (requires significant judgment on depreciation)

## Private Company Considerations

### Practical Challenges

| Challenge | Consideration |
|-----------|---------------|
| Limited market data | May need to rely more on Level 3 inputs |
| Valuation expertise | Consider engaging specialists |
| Cost-benefit | Focus resources on significant measurements |

### Common Fair Value Measurements

1. **Goodwill impairment testing** — Reporting unit fair value
2. **Stock compensation** — Private company stock value
3. **Business combinations** — Acquired assets and liabilities
4. **Investments** — Equity securities, debt securities

---

## Common Implementation Issues

1. **Level classification** — Incorrectly placing measurements in higher level
2. **Unit of account** — Measuring at wrong unit (individual asset vs. group)
3. **Highest and best use** — Using entity-specific use instead of market participant view
4. **Blockage factors** — Applying discount for large holdings (generally not permitted for Level 1)
5. **Own credit risk** — Failing to include in liability fair value
6. **Disclosure completeness** — Missing Level 3 reconciliation or sensitivity analysis

---

## Comparison: Fair Value vs. Other Measurements

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|------------|
| **Fair value** | Exit price in orderly transaction |
| **Fair value less costs to sell** | Fair value minus incremental direct selling costs |
| **Value in use** | Present value of cash flows from continuing use |
| **Net realizable value** | Estimated selling price minus completion/selling costs |
| **Transaction price** | Price paid or received |

---

## Recent Developments

### ASU 2024-02: Crypto Assets

Certain crypto assets now measured at fair value with changes in earnings.

### ASU 2022-03: Fair Value of Equity Securities with Contractual Sale Restrictions

Clarifies that restrictions on sale are not considered when measuring fair value.
