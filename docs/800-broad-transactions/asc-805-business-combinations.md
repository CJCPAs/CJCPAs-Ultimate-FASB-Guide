# ASC 805: Business Combinations

> Accounting for acquisitions using the acquisition method.

## Overview

ASC 805 requires that all business combinations be accounted for using the acquisition method. This approach results in recognizing the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed at their fair values as of the acquisition date, with any excess purchase price over net assets recognized as goodwill.

**Core Principle:**
An acquirer of a business recognizes the identifiable assets acquired, the liabilities assumed, and any noncontrolling interest in the acquiree at their acquisition-date fair values.

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Transactions in which an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses
- Combinations achieved by contract alone (stapling arrangements)
- Combinations achieved in stages (step acquisitions)

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Formation of joint ventures
- Acquisition of assets that do not constitute a business
- Combinations of entities under common control (apply ASC 805-50)
- Not-for-profit combinations (apply ASC 958-805)

---

## Business vs. Asset Acquisition

### Definition of a Business

A business is an integrated set of activities and assets capable of being conducted and managed to provide goods or services to customers, generate investment income, or generate other income from ordinary activities.

### ASU 2017-01: Clarifying the Definition

**Screen Test (Optional):**
If substantially all fair value is concentrated in a single identifiable asset (or group of similar assets), the set is NOT a business.

**If screen not met, evaluate:**
- Does the set include an input AND a substantive process that together significantly contribute to the ability to create outputs?

### Why It Matters

| Business Combination | Asset Acquisition |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| Goodwill recognized | No goodwill |
| Fair value allocation to all assets | Cost allocated based on relative fair value |
| Transaction costs expensed | Transaction costs capitalized |
| Contingent consideration at fair value | Contingent consideration only when paid |
| Deferred taxes recorded | No deferred taxes on initial recognition |

## The Acquisition Method

### Step 1: Identify the Acquirer

The acquirer is the entity that obtains control of the acquiree.

**Factors to Consider:**
- Relative voting rights after combination
- Existence of large minority voting interest
- Composition of governing body
- Composition of senior management
- Terms of equity exchange
- Relative size

### Step 2: Determine the Acquisition Date

The date on which the acquirer obtains control of the acquiree.

**Generally:** The closing date (when consideration transferred, assets acquired, liabilities assumed)

### Step 3: Recognize and Measure Identifiable Assets and Liabilities

**Recognition Criteria:**
Must meet definition of asset/liability at acquisition date (not necessarily recognition criteria under other GAAP).

**Measurement:**
Generally at fair value as of acquisition date.

**Exceptions to Fair Value:**
| Item | Measurement |
|------|-------------|
| Income taxes | ASC 740 (not fair value) |
| Employee benefits | ASC 712, 715 |
| Indemnification assets | Same as indemnified item |
| Reacquired rights | Remaining contractual term (no renewals) |
| Share-based awards | ASC 718 for replacement awards |
| Assets held for sale | Fair value less costs to sell |
| Leases | Per ASC 842 |

### Step 4: Recognize and Measure Goodwill or Bargain Purchase Gain

**Goodwill Formula:**
```
Goodwill = Consideration Transferred
         + Fair Value of Noncontrolling Interest (if any)
         + Fair Value of Previously Held Equity Interest (if any)
         - Net Identifiable Assets Acquired at Fair Value
```

**Bargain Purchase:**
If the above calculation results in a negative number:
1. Reassess whether all assets and liabilities are identified
2. Review measurement procedures
3. If still negative, recognize gain in earnings

## Consideration Transferred

### Types of Consideration

| Type | Measurement |
|------|-------------|
| Cash | Amount paid |
| Equity instruments | Fair value at acquisition date |
| Contingent consideration | Fair value at acquisition date |
| Previously held equity interest | Fair value at acquisition date |

### Contingent Consideration

**Initial Recognition:**
Fair value at acquisition date (even if payment not probable)

**Subsequent Accounting:**

| Classification | Treatment |
|----------------|-----------|
| Liability (most common) | Remeasure to fair value; changes in earnings |
| Equity | No remeasurement |
| Asset | Remeasure under appropriate GAAP |

---

## Identifiable Intangible Assets

### Recognition Criteria

Intangible asset is identifiable if it:
1. Is separable (can be sold, licensed, transferred), OR
2. Arises from contractual or legal rights

### Common Intangibles Acquired

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Marketing-related** | Trademarks, trade names, domain names |
| **Customer-related** | Customer lists, customer relationships, backlog |
| **Artistic-related** | Copyrights, photographs |
| **Contract-based** | Licensing agreements, franchise agreements, employment contracts |
| **Technology-based** | Patents, trade secrets, software |

### Assembled Workforce

Generally NOT recognized separately (subsumed into goodwill) because it does not meet the separability or contractual-legal criterion.

---

## Noncontrolling Interest (NCI)

### Measurement Options

| Method | Measurement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Full goodwill** | NCI at fair value (includes NCI's share of goodwill) |
| **Partial goodwill** | NCI at proportionate share of identifiable net assets (no goodwill to NCI) |

**Election:** Made on transaction-by-transaction basis.

### Subsequent Accounting

- Present NCI in equity, separate from parent's equity
- Attribute net income/comprehensive income to NCI
- Changes in parent's ownership while retaining control → equity transactions

---

## Step Acquisitions

### Previously Held Equity Interest

When an acquirer obtains control through a step acquisition:

1. **Remeasure** previously held equity interest to fair value at acquisition date
2. **Recognize gain/loss** in earnings
3. Reclassify any amounts in AOCI related to the investment to earnings

**Journal Entry (simplified):**
```
Dr. Investment (to fair value)    $XXX
    Cr. Gain on Step Acquisition         $XXX
```

---

## Measurement Period

### Definition

Period after acquisition date during which acquirer may adjust provisional amounts.

**Maximum:** One year from acquisition date

### Requirements

- New information about facts and circumstances that existed at acquisition date
- Retrospectively adjust provisional amounts
- If new information relates to events after acquisition date, record prospectively

---

## Transaction Costs

### Treatment

**Expense as incurred** (not part of consideration transferred)

| Cost Type | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Investment banker fees | Expense |
| Legal fees | Expense |
| Accounting fees | Expense |
| Valuation fees | Expense |
| Costs to issue debt | Capitalize per ASC 470 |
| Costs to issue equity | Reduce equity proceeds per ASC 505 |

---

## Pushdown Accounting

### Definition

Recording in the acquiree's separate financial statements the fair value adjustments from the acquisition.

### Requirements (ASU 2014-17)

- **Option** for acquired entity to apply pushdown accounting
- Can elect upon change-in-control event
- Once elected, cannot be reversed

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Basic Acquisition with Goodwill

**Facts:**
- Acquirer pays $10,000,000 cash for 100% of Target
- Fair value of identifiable assets: $12,000,000
- Fair value of liabilities assumed: $4,000,000

**Calculation:**
Net identifiable assets: $12,000,000 - $4,000,000 = $8,000,000
Goodwill: $10,000,000 - $8,000,000 = $2,000,000

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Identifiable Assets          $12,000,000
Dr. Goodwill                     $2,000,000
    Cr. Liabilities Assumed              $4,000,000
    Cr. Cash                             $10,000,000
```

---

### Example 2: Acquisition with Contingent Consideration

**Facts:**
- Cash paid at closing: $8,000,000
- Contingent payment: $2,000,000 if EBITDA exceeds $5M in Year 1
- Fair value of contingent consideration at acquisition: $1,200,000
- Net identifiable assets at fair value: $7,500,000

**Total Consideration:**
$8,000,000 + $1,200,000 = $9,200,000

**Goodwill:**
$9,200,000 - $7,500,000 = $1,700,000

**At Acquisition:**
```
Dr. Identifiable Assets          $XX,XXX,XXX
Dr. Goodwill                     $1,700,000
    Cr. Liabilities Assumed              $XX,XXX,XXX
    Cr. Cash                             $8,000,000
    Cr. Contingent Consideration Liability $1,200,000
```

**Year 1 (if EBITDA achieved and $2M paid):**
```
Dr. Contingent Consideration Liability $1,200,000
Dr. Change in Fair Value of Contingent 
    Consideration (Loss)         $800,000
    Cr. Cash                             $2,000,000
```

---

### Example 3: Acquisition with NCI (Full Goodwill Method)

**Facts:**
- Acquirer purchases 80% of Target for $8,000,000
- Fair value of 20% NCI: $2,000,000
- Fair value of identifiable net assets: $9,000,000

**Total Fair Value:**
$8,000,000 + $2,000,000 = $10,000,000

**Goodwill:**
$10,000,000 - $9,000,000 = $1,000,000

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Identifiable Net Assets      $9,000,000
Dr. Goodwill                     $1,000,000
    Cr. Cash                             $8,000,000
    Cr. Noncontrolling Interest          $2,000,000
```

## Private Company Considerations

### PCC Alternatives

| Topic | Alternative |
|-------|-------------|
| **Goodwill** | Amortize straight-line over 10 years (or less); simplified impairment |
| **Intangibles** | Subsume certain intangibles (customer-related, noncompete) into goodwill |

### Practical Implications

- Reduces complexity of intangible asset identification
- Reduces ongoing impairment testing burden
- Must be applied consistently

---

## Disclosure Requirements

### Required Disclosures

1. **Name and description** of acquiree
2. **Acquisition date**
3. **Percentage acquired**
4. **Primary reasons** for the combination
5. **How control was obtained**
6. **Fair value of consideration** (by type)
7. **Acquisition-date amounts** for major classes of assets/liabilities
8. **Contingent consideration** arrangements
9. **Goodwill:**
   - Amount expected to be tax deductible
   - Reporting unit(s) assigned
   - Qualitative factors contributing to goodwill
10. **Bargain purchase** (if applicable): Amount and reasons

### Measurement Period Adjustments

Disclose nature and amount of adjustments in period recognized.

---

## Common Implementation Issues

1. **Business definition** — Not applying screen test correctly
2. **Intangible identification** — Missing separable intangibles
3. **Contingent consideration** — Not remeasuring liability-classified amounts
4. **Transaction costs** — Capitalizing instead of expensing
5. **Measurement period** — Adjusting for events after acquisition date
6. **NCI measurement** — Inconsistent application of election
