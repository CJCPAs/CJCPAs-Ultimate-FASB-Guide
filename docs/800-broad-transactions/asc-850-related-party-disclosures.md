# ASC 850: Related Party Disclosures

> Essential guidance for identifying and disclosing transactions with parties that may not be at arm's length.

## Overview

ASC 850 establishes requirements for disclosing related party relationships and transactions. Related party transactions are significant because they may not be conducted at arm's length—the terms might differ from those that would exist between unrelated parties. Understanding and properly disclosing these relationships is critical for financial statement transparency.

**Core Principle:**
> "Financial statements shall include disclosures of material related party transactions, except compensation arrangements, expense allowances, and other similar items in the ordinary course of business."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- All entities preparing financial statements in conformity with GAAP
- Transactions between related parties
- Relationships with related parties (even without transactions)

### Exemptions from Disclosure:
- Compensation arrangements and expense allowances in ordinary course
- Transactions eliminated in consolidated or combined financial statements
- Separate financial statements of subsidiaries if presented with consolidated statements

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Related Parties** | Parties with ability to control or significantly influence the other party |
| **Affiliate** | Party that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control |
| **Control** | Possession of power to direct management and policies |
| **Immediate Family** | Family members who might control or influence, or be controlled by, related party |
| **Principal Owners** | Owners of more than 10% voting interest |
| **Management** | Directors, CEO, COO, CFO, VP and above, or those with policy-making responsibility |
| **Arm's Length Transaction** | Transaction conducted as if parties were unrelated |

---

## Identifying Related Parties

### Categories of Related Parties

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Affiliates** | Parent, subsidiaries, sister companies |
| **Principal owners** | > 10% shareholders and their immediate family |
| **Management** | Officers, directors, and their immediate family |
| **Equity method investees** | 20-50% owned entities |
| **Employee benefit trusts** | Pension plans, ESOP trusts |
| **Other parties** | Entities that can significantly influence |

### Common Related Party Relationships

| Relationship | Description |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Parent-subsidiary** | Direct control through ownership |
| **Brother-sister companies** | Common ownership/control |
| **Investor-investee** | Significant influence without control |
| **Management-entity** | Officers/directors and their entities |
| **Family relationships** | Entities owned by family members |

### Identification Procedures

**Management should:**
1. Maintain list of known related parties
2. Review board minutes for transactions
3. Obtain management representations
4. Review ownership records
5. Examine major contracts for related party indicators
6. Inquire of management about relationships

## Types of Related Party Transactions

### Common Transaction Types

| Transaction Type | Examples |
|------------------|----------|
| **Sales/purchases** | Goods or services between related parties |
| **Loans/advances** | Intercompany financing |
| **Leases** | Property leased from/to related parties |
| **Guarantees** | Debt guaranteed by related party |
| **Management fees** | Fees for services provided |
| **Royalties** | IP licensing arrangements |
| **Equity transactions** | Stock issuances, redemptions |

### Transactions Requiring Special Attention

| Transaction | Concern |
|-------------|---------|
| **Below-market loans** | Imputed interest may be required |
| **Above-market purchases** | May indicate disguised distributions |
| **Rent different from market** | May overstate/understate expenses |
| **Forgiveness of debt** | May be capital transaction |
| **Sales without substance** | Revenue recognition concerns |

---

## Disclosure Requirements

### When Disclosure Is Required

Disclose related party transactions if:
- Material to the financial statements
- Necessary for fair presentation
- Needed to prevent misleading statements

### Required Disclosures

| Element | Disclosure |
|---------|------------|
| **Nature of relationship** | How parties are related |
| **Description of transaction** | What was exchanged |
| **Dollar amounts** | Transaction amounts and balances |
| **Amounts due to/from** | Outstanding balances |
| **Terms and manner of settlement** | Payment terms, conditions |
| **Other information** | Necessary for understanding |

### Disclosure Even Without Transactions

**Control relationships** must be disclosed even if no transactions occurred:

| Situation | Required Disclosure |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Common ownership | Nature of control relationship |
| Common management | If could affect operating results |
| Ability to influence | Even without actual transactions |

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Comprehensive Related Party Note Disclosure

```
NOTE X: RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS

The Company has the following related party relationships and transactions:

OWNERSHIP AND CONTROL
The Company is a wholly-owned subsidiary of ABC Holdings, Inc.
The Company shares common ownership with XYZ Services, LLC ("XYZ").

TRANSACTIONS WITH PARENT
During 20X1, the Company paid management fees to ABC Holdings of
$500,000 (20X0: $450,000) for shared services including IT support,
human resources, and treasury functions. At December 31, 20X1,
$45,000 was payable to ABC Holdings (20X0: $40,000).

TRANSACTIONS WITH SISTER COMPANY
The Company purchases certain raw materials from XYZ. Total purchases
during 20X1 were $1,200,000 (20X0: $980,000). Management believes
these transactions are at terms comparable to those available from
unrelated vendors. At December 31, 20X1, $180,000 was payable to XYZ
(20X0: $150,000).

LOANS FROM SHAREHOLDERS
The Company has a note payable to John Smith, a 30% shareholder, in
the amount of $250,000, bearing interest at 5% per annum, due
December 31, 20X5. Interest expense was $12,500 for 20X1.

LEASE ARRANGEMENTS
The Company leases its headquarters facility from Smith Properties,
LLC, an entity controlled by the Company's CEO. Annual rent is
$240,000, and the lease expires December 31, 20X8. Future minimum
lease payments total $720,000.
```

### Example 2: Below-Market Loan from Shareholder

**Facts:**
- Shareholder loans company $500,000
- Stated interest: 0%
- Term: 5 years
- Market rate: 6%

**Analysis:**
Per ASC 835-30, impute interest at market rate.

**Present Value Calculation:**
- PV = $500,000 / (1.06)^5 = $373,629
- Discount = $500,000 - $373,629 = $126,371

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Cash                             $500,000
    Cr. Note Payable—Shareholder              $500,000

Dr. Note Payable—Shareholder         $126,371
    Cr. Additional Paid-in Capital            $126,371
(Record capital contribution for below-market rate)
```

**Annual Interest Accrual (Year 1):**
```
Dr. Interest Expense                  $22,418
    Cr. Note Payable—Shareholder              $22,418
($373,629 × 6%)
```

**Disclosure:**
"During 20X1, the Company received a $500,000 non-interest bearing loan from a shareholder. The loan has been recorded at its present value of $373,629 using a discount rate of 6%, with the $126,371 difference recorded as a capital contribution. The loan matures December 31, 20X5."

---

### Example 3: Related Party Lease

**Facts:**
- Company leases building from entity owned by CEO
- Monthly rent: $15,000 ($180,000 annually)
- Market rent: $12,000 monthly ($144,000 annually)
- Lease term: 10 years

**Analysis:**
The above-market rent may represent additional compensation to the CEO.

**Journal Entry (Monthly):**
```
Dr. Rent Expense                      $15,000
    Cr. Cash                                   $15,000
```

**Alternative Treatment (if appropriate):**
```
Dr. Rent Expense                      $12,000
Dr. Compensation Expense               $3,000
    Cr. Cash                                   $15,000
(If above-market portion deemed compensation)
```

**Disclosure:**
"The Company leases its principal office from a limited liability company owned by the Company's CEO. Annual rent under this lease is $180,000 through 20X8. Management has evaluated comparable properties and believes the rental rate may exceed market rates by approximately $36,000 annually."

---

### Example 4: Intercompany Sales

**Facts:**
- Parent company sells inventory to subsidiary
- Transfer price: $1,000,000 (cost: $700,000)
- Subsidiary holds $200,000 at year-end

**Consolidation Entries:**

**Eliminate intercompany sale:**
```
Dr. Sales—Parent                   $1,000,000
    Cr. Cost of Sales—Parent                 $700,000
    Cr. Inventory—Subsidiary                 $300,000
(Eliminate intercompany sale; defer unrealized profit)
```

**Note:** While eliminated in consolidation, may require disclosure in subsidiary's standalone statements.

## Special Situations

### Variable Interest Entities (VIEs)

Related party analysis is critical for VIE determination:
- Related party groups may be considered single decision maker
- Requires careful analysis of relationships
- See ASC 810 for consolidation guidance

### Common Control Transactions

**Transactions between entities under common control:**
- Often recorded at carrying value (not fair value)
- Disclosed as related party
- Special rules for business combinations under common control

### Management Compensation

**Generally NOT disclosed as related party:**
- Ordinary compensation arrangements
- Standard expense allowances
- Typical employee benefits

**MAY require disclosure if:**
- Unusual or material
- Outside normal compensation
- Could affect understanding of financial statements

## Private Company Considerations

### Enhanced Importance

Related party transactions are often more prevalent and significant for private companies:
- Owner-operated businesses
- Family-owned enterprises
- Closely held structures

### Common Private Company Related Party Issues

| Issue | Consideration |
|-------|---------------|
| **Personal expenses** | Owner expenses paid by company |
| **Below-market rent** | Company using owner's property |
| **Loans to/from owners** | Documentation and terms |
| **Family employment** | Compensation reasonableness |
| **Related entity transactions** | Multiple entities under common control |

### Documentation Requirements

Private companies should maintain:
- Written agreements for major transactions
- Board/member approval documentation
- Market rate support (comps, appraisals)
- Transfer pricing documentation

## Common Audit Issues

### Identification Challenges

| Issue | Audit Response |
|-------|----------------|
| **Undisclosed relationships** | Expanded inquiry procedures |
| **Complex ownership** | Review organizational charts |
| **Indirect relationships** | Trace through intermediaries |
| **Family relationships** | Confirm with management |

### Terms and Conditions

| Issue | Consideration |
|-------|---------------|
| **Arms-length assertion** | Require supporting evidence |
| **Market rate claims** | Obtain comparable data |
| **Unusual terms** | Question business purpose |
| **Verbal agreements** | Encourage written documentation |

### Disclosure Completeness

**Verify:**
- All material transactions disclosed
- Nature of relationship clear
- Dollar amounts accurate
- Terms adequately described
- Control relationships disclosed

## Disclosure Checklist

### Required Elements

- [ ] Nature of relationship
- [ ] Description of transaction(s)
- [ ] Dollar amounts of transactions
- [ ] Amounts due to or from related parties
- [ ] Terms and manner of settlement
- [ ] Control relationships (even without transactions)

### Consider Disclosing

- [ ] Whether terms comparable to arm's length
- [ ] Business purpose of arrangement
- [ ] Duration/renewal of arrangements
- [ ] Any guarantees or collateral
- [ ] Method of pricing determination

## Sample Disclosures by Transaction Type

### Loan to/from Shareholder

```
The Company has a $300,000 note receivable from its majority
shareholder bearing interest at the prime rate (5.5% at December 31,
20X1). Interest income for 20X1 was $16,500. The note is due on
demand but management does not expect to call the loan within the
next twelve months. The note is classified as noncurrent.
```

### Management Fees

```
Under a management services agreement, the Company pays monthly fees
to its parent company for administrative services including
accounting, legal, and information technology support. Total fees
for 20X1 were $720,000 (20X0: $680,000). The agreement may be
terminated by either party with 90 days' notice.
```

### Related Party Purchases

```
The Company purchases substantially all of its raw materials from
ABC Supply Co., a company owned by the brother of the Company's CEO.
Total purchases were $2.4 million in 20X1 and $2.1 million in 20X0.
At December 31, 20X1, accounts payable to ABC Supply totaled
$320,000. Management believes the pricing and terms are consistent
with those available from unrelated suppliers.
```
