# ASC 852: Reorganizations

> Fresh-start reporting and accounting for entities in Chapter 11 bankruptcy.

## Overview

ASC 852 provides guidance on financial reporting by entities that have filed a petition for relief under the Bankruptcy Code (Chapter 11) and for entities that emerge from Chapter 11 reorganization. The guidance includes fresh-start reporting requirements when applicable.

> **Core Principle:** Entities emerging from bankruptcy may be required to adopt fresh-start reporting, which essentially creates a new reporting entity with assets and liabilities at fair value and no retained earnings.

---

## Scope

### Applies To

- Entities that have filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy
- Entities emerging from Chapter 11 reorganization
- Entities in similar reorganization proceedings

### Does NOT Apply To

- Chapter 7 liquidations (entity will not continue)
- Troubled debt restructuring outside bankruptcy (ASC 470-60)
- General going concern issues (ASC 205-40)

---

## During Chapter 11 Proceedings

### Financial Reporting Requirements

**Continue preparing financial statements** during bankruptcy with specific modifications:

| Requirement | Treatment |
|-------------|-----------|
| Going concern | Evaluate and disclose |
| Segregation | Separate pre-petition from post-petition |
| Liabilities | Classify subject to compromise |
| Professional fees | Expense as incurred |
| Interest | Accrue only if payment is probable |

### Liabilities Subject to Compromise

**Definition:** Pre-petition liabilities that may be impaired by the bankruptcy proceeding.

**Presentation:**
- Report separately on balance sheet
- At expected allowed claim amount
- Not at fair value

**Examples:**
- Pre-petition accounts payable
- Pre-petition debt
- Estimated rejected contract claims
- Litigation claims

### Journal Entry—Reclassify to Subject to Compromise

```
Dr. Pre-Petition Accounts Payable         $500,000
Dr. Pre-Petition Long-Term Debt         $2,000,000
    Cr. Liabilities Subject to Compromise      $2,500,000
```

---

## Reorganization Items

### Definition

Income, expenses, gains, or losses directly related to the reorganization.

### Presentation

Report **separately** in income statement, net of tax:
- Within income from continuing operations
- After income from operations

### Examples of Reorganization Items

| Item | Classification |
|------|---------------|
| Professional fees (legal, financial advisors) | Reorganization expense |
| Gains on debt discharge | Reorganization gain |
| Losses on asset dispositions (reorganization-related) | Reorganization loss |
| Interest income on accumulated cash | Reorganization income |
| Fresh-start adjustments | Reorganization item |
| Rejected lease claims | Reorganization item |

### Income Statement Presentation

```
Income from operations                    $XX,XXX
Reorganization items, net:
  Professional fees              $(500,000)
  Gain on settlement of claims   $800,000
  Fresh-start adjustments        $200,000
    Net reorganization items               $500,000
                                          --------
Income before taxes                       $XX,XXX
```

## Fresh-Start Reporting

### When Required

Fresh-start reporting is required when **BOTH** conditions are met:

| Condition | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Reorganization value test** | Reorganization value < Total post-petition liabilities + Pre-petition claims |
| **Ownership change test** | Pre-petition owners receive < 50% of emerging entity's voting shares |

### Plain English

1. The company is worth less than what it owes, AND
2. The old shareholders are getting less than half of the new company

### Reorganization Value

**Definition:** The value of the entity's assets without considering liabilities—essentially enterprise value as of emergence.

**Determination methods:**
- Discounted cash flow analysis
- Market multiples
- Comparable transaction analysis
- Liquidation analysis (floor)

## Fresh-Start Accounting

### Key Principles

When fresh-start reporting applies:

1. **New reporting entity** — Treat emergence as a new company
2. **Fair value** — Measure all assets and liabilities at fair value
3. **No retained earnings** — Emerge with zero retained earnings
4. **Goodwill** — May arise from reorganization value exceeding net assets

### Steps to Apply Fresh-Start

**Step 1:** Determine reorganization value (enterprise value)

**Step 2:** Allocate to assets and liabilities at fair value (similar to business combination)

**Step 3:** Record goodwill (or bargain purchase) if reorganization value differs from net assets

**Step 4:** Eliminate predecessor retained earnings (deficit)

**Step 5:** Establish new equity structure

### Fresh-Start Journal Entry

**Scenario:**
- Reorganization value: $10,000,000
- Fair value of identifiable assets: $15,000,000
- Fair value of liabilities: $8,000,000
- Net identifiable assets: $7,000,000
- Pre-petition equity (deficit): $(5,000,000)

**Goodwill:** $10,000,000 − $7,000,000 = $3,000,000

```
Dr. Identifiable Assets (FV adjustment)   $2,000,000
Dr. Goodwill                              $3,000,000
Dr. Liabilities (reduction to FV)         $1,000,000
    Cr. Retained Earnings (Deficit)              $5,000,000
    Cr. Additional Paid-In Capital               $1,000,000
```

## Gain on Debt Discharge

### When Recognized

Recognize gain when:
- Plan of reorganization is confirmed
- Debt is legally discharged or modified

### Measurement

```
Gain = Carrying Amount of Debt − Amount Paid/New Instrument Fair Value
```

### Example

**Pre-petition debt:**
- Principal: $5,000,000
- Accrued interest: $500,000
- Total: $5,500,000

**Settlement:** Creditors receive $2,000,000 cash + 30% of new equity (FV $1,500,000)

**Gain on discharge:** $5,500,000 − $3,500,000 = **$2,000,000**

```
Dr. Liabilities Subject to Compromise    $5,500,000
    Cr. Cash                                    $2,000,000
    Cr. Common Stock (new)                      $1,500,000
    Cr. Gain on Debt Discharge                  $2,000,000
```

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: During Bankruptcy

**Company files Chapter 11 with:**
- Pre-petition debt: $10M
- Pre-petition payables: $2M
- Post-petition DIP financing: $3M

**Balance Sheet Presentation:**

| Liabilities | Amount |
|-------------|--------|
| Post-petition accounts payable | $500,000 |
| DIP financing | $3,000,000 |
| **Liabilities subject to compromise:** | |
| Pre-petition debt | $10,000,000 |
| Pre-petition accounts payable | $2,000,000 |
| **Total liabilities** | **$15,500,000** |

### Example 2: Fresh-Start Application

**Emergence data:**
| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Reorganization value | $50M |
| Identifiable assets (at FV) | $60M |
| Liabilities (at FV) | $20M |
| Net identifiable assets | $40M |

**Goodwill:** $50M − $40M = $10M

**Or Bargain Purchase if:**
- Net identifiable assets > Reorganization value
- Results in gain (unusual in bankruptcy)

## Disclosure Requirements

### During Bankruptcy

1. **Petition date** and case description
2. **Major conditions** of plan (if confirmed)
3. **Reorganization items** by major type
4. **Cash used** for reorganization items
5. **Claims** filed vs. scheduled liabilities

### At Emergence (Fresh-Start)

1. **Adjustments** to historical carrying amounts
2. **Reorganization value** and how determined
3. **Debt discharge** and related gains
4. **Post-emergence** capital structure
5. **Fresh-start adjustments** explanation

### Example Disclosure

> **Reorganization:** On [date], the Company filed a voluntary petition for relief under Chapter 11 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code. The plan of reorganization was confirmed on [date] and became effective on [date]. Upon emergence, the Company adopted fresh-start reporting in accordance with ASC 852. The reorganization value of approximately $X million was determined using a discounted cash flow analysis.
>
> In connection with emergence, the Company recorded reorganization items of $X million, consisting primarily of gains on debt discharge of $X million, partially offset by professional fees of $X million and fresh-start adjustments of $X million.

## Private Company Considerations

### Applicability

- Same guidance applies to private companies
- Often smaller, simpler capital structures
- May have fewer creditor classes

### Practical Issues

1. Determining reorganization value
2. Valuation of intangible assets
3. Equity valuation for ownership test
4. Professional fee tracking

---

## Common Audit Issues

1. **Fresh-start threshold** — Incorrect application of two-condition test
2. **Reorganization value** — Inadequate support for valuation
3. **Fair value measurements** — Level 3 inputs not documented
4. **Timing** — Recognition before plan confirmation
5. **Classification** — Liabilities not properly segregated
6. **Disclosures** — Incomplete bankruptcy-related disclosures
7. **Reorganization items** — Improper classification of expenses

---

## Comparison: Fresh-Start vs. No Fresh-Start

| Aspect | Fresh-Start | No Fresh-Start |
|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Assets | Fair value | Historical cost (adjusted) |
| Liabilities | Fair value | Settlement amounts |
| Retained earnings | Zero | Continues (adjusted for gains) |
| Goodwill | May recognize | No new goodwill |
| Comparability | New entity | Continues |

---

## Tax Considerations

### Cancellation of Debt Income

- Generally taxable income
- **Exception:** Title 11 bankruptcy exclusion
- Attribute reduction required

### Fresh-Start Tax Basis

- May differ from GAAP fresh-start values
- Creates deferred tax implications
- Complex interaction with NOL carryforwards
