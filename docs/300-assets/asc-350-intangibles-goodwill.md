# ASC 350: Intangibles—Goodwill and Other

> The comprehensive standard for accounting for goodwill and other intangible assets.

## Overview

ASC 350 provides guidance on the accounting for goodwill and other intangible assets, including initial recognition, subsequent measurement, amortization (for finite-lived intangibles), and impairment testing. The standard distinguishes between goodwill, indefinite-lived intangibles, and finite-lived intangibles.

**Core Principle:**
> "Goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets are not amortized but are tested for impairment at least annually. Finite-lived intangible assets are amortized over their useful lives and tested for impairment when events indicate potential impairment."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Goodwill acquired in business combinations
- Intangible assets acquired individually or in groups
- Internally developed intangible assets (limited)
- Internal-use software (ASC 350-40)
- Website development costs (ASC 350-50)

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Intangible assets acquired in a business combination (initial recognition—ASC 805)
- Financial assets (ASC 320, 321, 323, 325)
- Deferred tax assets (ASC 740)
- Leases (ASC 842)
- Servicing assets (ASC 860)

---

## Key Definitions

### Goodwill

The excess of the consideration transferred over the fair value of net identifiable assets acquired in a business combination.

**Characteristics:**
- Arises only from business combinations
- Represents future economic benefits from assets not individually identified
- Cannot be sold separately from the business
- Assigned to reporting units

### Intangible Assets

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|------------|----------|
| **Indefinite-lived** | No foreseeable limit on cash flow generation | Certain trademarks, broadcast licenses |
| **Finite-lived** | Limited period of benefit | Patents, customer relationships, non-compete agreements |

### Reporting Unit

A component of an operating segment that:
- Constitutes a business
- Has discrete financial information available
- Is regularly reviewed by segment management

---

## Initial Recognition

### Acquired Intangibles

**Recognition Criteria (from business combination):**
- Arises from contractual or legal rights, OR
- Is separable (can be sold, transferred, licensed, rented, or exchanged)

**Measurement:** Fair value at acquisition date

### Common Intangible Assets Acquired

| Category | Examples | Typical Life |
|----------|----------|--------------|
| **Marketing-related** | Trademarks, trade names, internet domains | Indefinite or finite |
| **Customer-related** | Customer lists, relationships, contracts, backlog | Finite (2-15 years) |
| **Artistic-related** | Copyrights, literary works, musical compositions | Finite |
| **Contract-based** | Licensing agreements, franchise rights, permits | Contract term |
| **Technology-based** | Patents, trade secrets, software, databases | Finite (3-10 years) |

### Internally Developed Intangibles

**General Rule:** Expense as incurred

**Exceptions:**
- Internal-use software (ASC 350-40)
- Website development (ASC 350-50)
- Costs of obtaining patents (capitalize legal costs)

## Internal-Use Software (ASC 350-40)

### Development Stages

| Stage | Accounting | Examples |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Preliminary project stage** | Expense | Conceptual design, vendor evaluation, requirements gathering |
| **Application development stage** | Capitalize | Coding, testing, installation, data conversion |
| **Post-implementation stage** | Expense | Training, maintenance, minor upgrades |

### Capitalization Criteria

Capitalize costs when:
1. Preliminary project stage is complete
2. Management authorizes and commits to funding
3. It is probable the project will be completed
4. Software will be used as intended

### Costs to Capitalize

| Capitalize | Expense |
|------------|---------|
| External direct costs (coding, testing) | Preliminary stage costs |
| Payroll costs directly associated | Training costs |
| Interest costs (if qualifying asset) | Data conversion (general/ongoing) |
| Upgrades adding functionality | Maintenance |

### Amortization

- Begin when software is ready for intended use
- Straight-line method (unless another method more appropriate)
- Useful life typically 3-7 years

---

## Website Development Costs (ASC 350-50)

### Development Stages

| Stage | Accounting | Activities |
|-------|------------|------------|
| **Planning** | Expense | Conceptual design, feature evaluation |
| **Website application and infrastructure** | Capitalize | Acquiring/developing software, graphics |
| **Graphics development** | Capitalize | Design of overall look |
| **Content development** | Generally expense | Entering content (some exceptions) |
| **Operating** | Expense | Training, administration, updates |

## Amortization of Finite-Lived Intangibles

### Method

- Use method reflecting pattern of economic benefit consumption
- If pattern cannot be reliably determined, use straight-line
- Review useful life each reporting period

### Common Useful Lives

| Asset Type | Typical Life | Basis |
|------------|--------------|-------|
| Customer relationships | 5-15 years | Attrition analysis |
| Technology/patents | 3-10 years | Legal life or obsolescence |
| Non-compete agreements | Contract term | Agreement period |
| Trade names (finite) | 5-20 years | Market/brand analysis |
| Backlog | Months | Order fulfillment period |
| Franchise rights | Contract term | Agreement period |

### Journal Entry—Amortization

```
Dr. Amortization Expense           $XXX
    Cr. Accumulated Amortization—Intangible   $XXX
```

## Goodwill Impairment Testing

### Testing Frequency

- **Required:** At least annually (same time each year)
- **Triggered:** When events or circumstances indicate fair value may be below carrying amount

### Triggering Events

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Macroeconomic** | Deteriorating economic conditions, increased cost of capital |
| **Industry/market** | Declining market, increased competition, regulatory changes |
| **Cost factors** | Increased raw materials, labor, or other costs |
| **Financial performance** | Declining revenue, margins, or cash flows |
| **Entity-specific** | Change in key personnel, litigation, restructuring |
| **Events affecting reporting unit** | Sale or disposal of significant portion |

### Impairment Test—Quantitative Approach

**Step 1 (Only Step Now):**
Compare fair value of reporting unit to its carrying amount (including goodwill).

| Result | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Fair value > Carrying amount | No impairment |
| Fair value < Carrying amount | Recognize impairment loss |

**Impairment Loss =** Carrying amount − Fair value (limited to goodwill balance)

**Note:** Goodwill impairment cannot exceed the goodwill allocated to the reporting unit.

### Qualitative Assessment (Step 0)

Entities may first perform qualitative assessment to determine if quantitative test is necessary.

**Evaluate:** Whether it's more likely than not (>50%) that fair value is less than carrying amount.

**If yes:** Perform quantitative test
**If no:** No further testing required

### Fair Value Determination

| Approach | Description | Use |
|----------|-------------|-----|
| **Income approach** | DCF of expected future cash flows | Most common |
| **Market approach** | Comparable transactions, multiples | Corroborative |
| **Cost approach** | Rarely used for reporting units | Limited |

### Journal Entry—Goodwill Impairment

```
Dr. Goodwill Impairment Loss       $XXX
    Cr. Goodwill                            $XXX
```

**Presentation:** Separate line item in operating expenses (before subtotal if presenting one)

---

## Indefinite-Lived Intangible Impairment

### Testing Approach

Same framework as goodwill:
1. Optional qualitative assessment
2. Quantitative test: Compare fair value to carrying amount

### Key Difference from Finite-Lived

- Not amortized
- Must reassess useful life each period
- If life becomes finite, begin amortization prospectively

### Impairment Loss

**Loss =** Carrying amount − Fair value

```
Dr. Impairment Loss—Intangible     $XXX
    Cr. Intangible Asset                    $XXX
```

---

## Finite-Lived Intangible Impairment

### Framework

Apply ASC 360 (impairment of long-lived assets):

**Step 1: Recoverability Test**
Is carrying amount > Undiscounted future cash flows?

| Result | Action |
|--------|--------|
| No | No impairment |
| Yes | Proceed to Step 2 |

**Step 2: Measurement**
Impairment = Carrying amount − Fair value

## Private Company Alternatives

### Goodwill Accounting Alternative (ASC 350-20-35-62 through 65)

Private companies may elect:

**Amortization:**
- Amortize goodwill straight-line over 10 years (or less if appropriate)
- Useful life not to exceed 10 years

**Impairment Testing:**
- Test only when triggering event occurs (no annual test required)
- Test at entity level or reporting unit level (election)
- Qualitative assessment permitted

**Advantages:**
- Reduces complexity and cost
- No annual impairment testing
- Simpler impairment analysis

### Subsume Intangibles into Goodwill (ASC 805)

Private companies may elect to:
- Not separately recognize certain intangibles (customer-related intangibles, non-compete agreements)
- Subsume them into goodwill
- Results in higher goodwill subject to amortization

### Example: Private Company Goodwill

**Facts:**
- Acquired goodwill: $1,000,000
- Elected 10-year straight-line amortization
- No triggering events in Year 1

**Annual Entry:**
```
Dr. Amortization Expense           $100,000
    Cr. Goodwill                            $100,000
```

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Internal-Use Software Development

**Facts:**
- Preliminary project costs: $50,000
- Application development (coding, testing): $400,000
- Data conversion (one-time): $30,000
- Training costs: $25,000
- Implementation costs (go-live): $20,000

**Analysis:**

| Cost | Treatment | Amount |
|------|-----------|-------:|
| Preliminary project | Expense | $50,000 |
| Application development | Capitalize | $400,000 |
| Data conversion (one-time) | Capitalize | $30,000 |
| Training | Expense | $25,000 |
| Implementation | Capitalize | $20,000 |
| **Total Capitalized** | | **$450,000** |
| **Total Expensed** | | **$75,000** |

**Useful life:** 5 years
**Annual amortization:** $450,000 / 5 = $90,000

---

### Example 2: Goodwill Impairment—Quantitative Test

**Facts:**
- Reporting unit carrying amount: $5,000,000
- Including goodwill of: $1,500,000
- Fair value of reporting unit: $4,200,000

**Analysis:**

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|-------:|
| Carrying amount | $5,000,000 |
| Fair value | $4,200,000 |
| **Excess carrying amount** | **$800,000** |

**Impairment Loss:** $800,000 (limited to goodwill of $1,500,000)

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Goodwill Impairment Loss       $800,000
    Cr. Goodwill                            $800,000
```

**Remaining Goodwill:** $1,500,000 − $800,000 = $700,000

---

### Example 3: Acquired Intangible Assets

**Facts:**
Company acquires business with following identifiable intangibles:

| Asset | Fair Value | Useful Life |
|-------|----------:|-------------|
| Trade name | $500,000 | Indefinite |
| Customer relationships | $800,000 | 10 years |
| Developed technology | $300,000 | 5 years |
| Non-compete agreement | $100,000 | 3 years |

**Year 1 Amortization:**

| Asset | Calculation | Expense |
|-------|-------------|--------:|
| Trade name | Not amortized | $0 |
| Customer relationships | $800,000 / 10 | $80,000 |
| Developed technology | $300,000 / 5 | $60,000 |
| Non-compete | $100,000 / 3 | $33,333 |
| **Total** | | **$173,333** |

---

### Example 4: Qualitative Goodwill Assessment

**Facts:**
- Company performs annual goodwill impairment test
- Recent developments:
  - Revenue increased 5%
  - Industry outlook positive
  - No significant changes in key personnel
  - Cost structure stable
  - No adverse regulatory changes

**Qualitative Assessment:**

| Factor | Evaluation |
|--------|------------|
| Macroeconomic | Stable economy, low interest rates |
| Industry | Growing market, favorable trends |
| Financial performance | Exceeding budget |
| Entity-specific | Stable operations |
| Reporting unit events | None significant |

**Conclusion:** Not more likely than not that fair value is below carrying amount. No quantitative testing required.

## Disclosure Requirements

### Goodwill

| Disclosure | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| Carrying amount by segment | Beginning and ending balances |
| Changes during period | Acquisitions, impairments, disposals, FX effects |
| Accumulated impairment losses | Cumulative impairments by segment |
| Reporting units at risk | If fair value close to carrying amount |

### Intangible Assets

| Disclosure | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| Gross carrying amount | By major class |
| Accumulated amortization | By major class |
| Aggregate amortization expense | For period |
| Estimated future amortization | For next five years |
| Weighted-average useful life | By major class |

### Impairment

- Description of impaired asset
- Facts and circumstances leading to impairment
- Amount of impairment loss
- Method of determining fair value
- Segment affected

## Common Implementation Issues

### 1. Reporting Unit Identification
- Over-aggregation or under-segmentation
- Not updating for organizational changes
- Missing components that constitute businesses

### 2. Triggering Events
- Not monitoring for indicators between annual tests
- Ignoring industry or macroeconomic changes
- Delayed recognition of entity-specific events

### 3. Fair Value Measurement
- Unsupported assumptions in DCF models
- Inconsistency between projections and historical performance
- Not considering market participant perspective

### 4. Qualitative Assessment
- Insufficient documentation
- Over-reliance on qualitative factors
- Not considering cumulative impact of factors

### 5. Internal-Use Software
- Improper capitalization of training costs
- Capitalizing preliminary project costs
- Not beginning amortization timely

## Software Comparison

### Internal-Use (ASC 350-40) vs. External-Sale (ASC 985-20)

| Aspect | Internal-Use | External-Sale |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **Capitalize when** | Application development stage | Technological feasibility |
| **Amortization** | Useful life | Greater of ratio or straight-line |
| **Impairment** | ASC 360 (recoverability) | NRV comparison |
| **Common period** | 3-7 years | Product life cycle |
