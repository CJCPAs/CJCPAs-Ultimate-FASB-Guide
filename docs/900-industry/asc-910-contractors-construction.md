# ASC 910: Contractors—Construction

> Specialized accounting for construction contractors including long-term contracts and project accounting.

## Overview

ASC 910 provides industry-specific guidance for construction contractors, supplementing the general revenue recognition guidance in ASC 606. Construction contracts often span multiple reporting periods and involve significant estimates, making proper accounting critical.

> **Core Principle:** Construction contractors recognize revenue over time when they create or enhance an asset the customer controls, or create an asset with no alternative use and have an enforceable right to payment.

---

## Scope

### Applies To

- General contractors
- Subcontractors
- Engineering firms
- Construction managers
- Specialty trade contractors
- Design-build contractors

### Contract Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Fixed-price** | Agreed price regardless of costs |
| **Cost-plus** | Reimbursable costs plus fee |
| **Time-and-materials** | Labor rates plus materials |
| **Unit-price** | Price per unit of output |

---

## Revenue Recognition Under ASC 606

### Over-Time Recognition Criteria

Construction revenue is typically recognized **over time** when:

1. **Customer controls asset** — Work performed on customer's property, OR
2. **No alternative use** — Asset created has no alternative use AND contractor has enforceable right to payment for work completed

### Measuring Progress

| Method | Basis | When Used |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| **Cost-to-cost** | Costs incurred / Total estimated costs | Most common |
| **Units delivered** | Units completed / Total units | Unit-price contracts |
| **Survey of work** | Engineering estimates | Complex projects |
| **Time elapsed** | Time / Total duration | Service-type contracts |

### Cost-to-Cost Formula

```
Revenue Recognized = (Costs Incurred to Date / Total Estimated Costs) × Total Contract Price
```

---

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Basic Cost-to-Cost Method

**Contract Information:**
- Fixed-price contract: $10,000,000
- Estimated total costs: $8,000,000

**Year 1:**
- Costs incurred: $2,400,000

**Calculation:**
- Percentage complete: $2,400,000 / $8,000,000 = 30%
- Revenue: 30% × $10,000,000 = $3,000,000
- Gross profit: $3,000,000 − $2,400,000 = $600,000

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Contract Asset (Costs Incurred)      $2,400,000
Dr. Accounts Receivable                   $500,000
    Cr. Revenue                                $3,000,000
    (Progress billings assumed)
```

### Example 2: Change in Estimate

**Year 2—Revised estimate:**
- Total estimated costs now: $8,500,000
- Cumulative costs incurred: $5,950,000

**Calculation:**
- New percentage: $5,950,000 / $8,500,000 = 70%
- Cumulative revenue: 70% × $10,000,000 = $7,000,000
- Previously recognized: $3,000,000
- Year 2 revenue: $7,000,000 − $3,000,000 = $4,000,000

## Contract Costs

### Costs to Include

| Include | Exclude |
|---------|---------|
| Direct materials | General & administrative |
| Direct labor | Selling costs |
| Subcontractor costs | Wasted materials (abnormal) |
| Equipment costs | Idle equipment |
| Allocated indirect costs | Unallocated overhead |
| Project insurance | General insurance |

### Pre-Contract Costs

| Cost Type | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Bid costs** | Expense as incurred |
| **Costs incurred in anticipation** | Expense unless contract is probable |
| **Mobilization costs** | May be setup activities (capitalize) |

### Cost Incurred Journal Entry

```
Dr. Contract Costs                       $500,000
    Cr. Cash/Accounts Payable                   $500,000
```

## Contract Modifications

### Types

| Type | Description | Treatment |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Change order** | Customer-requested change | Modify if approved/probable |
| **Claim** | Additional compensation sought | Recognize if probable and estimable |
| **Back charges** | Charges to subcontractors | Reduce contract costs |

### Change Order Accounting

**When approved or probable:**

*Adjust transaction price:*
```
Revised Contract Price = Original Price + Change Order
```

*Recalculate percentage complete and revenue*

### Claims

**Recognition criteria:**
- Work performed
- Claim amount determinable
- Collection is probable
- Evidence supports claim

**Conservative approach:** Recognize only amount highly probable of collection

## Loss Contracts

### When Estimated Costs Exceed Contract Price

**Recognize total loss immediately** when identified:

```
Expected Loss = Total Estimated Costs − Total Contract Price
```

### Example—Loss Recognition

**Contract price:** $5,000,000
**Total estimated costs:** $5,500,000
**Expected loss:** $500,000

**Costs incurred to date:** $2,000,000
**Revenue recognized to date:** $1,818,182 (at percentage of 36.4%)

**Journal Entry—Recognize Full Loss:**
```
Dr. Loss on Construction Contract         $500,000
    Cr. Contract Liability                       $500,000
```

## Progress Billings

### Billing vs. Revenue

Progress billings may differ from revenue recognized:

| Situation | Balance Sheet |
|-----------|---------------|
| Revenue > Billings | Contract Asset (asset) |
| Billings > Revenue | Contract Liability |

### Journal Entries

**Progress billing to customer:**
```
Dr. Accounts Receivable                 $1,000,000
    Cr. Progress Billings                      $1,000,000
```

**Balance sheet presentation:**
```
If Revenue > Billings:
    Contract Asset = Revenue Recognized − Progress Billings

If Billings > Revenue:
    Contract Liability = Progress Billings − Revenue Recognized
```

---

## Retainage

### Definition

Portion of progress billing withheld by customer until contract completion.

### Accounting

**At billing:**
```
Dr. Retainage Receivable                 $100,000
Dr. Accounts Receivable                  $900,000
    Cr. Progress Billings                      $1,000,000
```

**At contract completion:**
```
Dr. Cash                                 $100,000
    Cr. Retainage Receivable                   $100,000
```

### Balance Sheet Presentation

- **Current** if expected within one year
- **Noncurrent** if collection beyond one year

## Joint Ventures and Combining Contracts

### Construction Joint Ventures

**Accounting depends on structure:**

| Structure | Accounting |
|-----------|------------|
| Separate legal entity | Equity method or consolidation |
| Undivided interest | Proportionate consolidation |
| Contractual arrangement | Gross or net, depending on control |

### Combining Contracts

Combine contracts negotiated as a package if:
- Negotiated as single package
- Single commercial objective
- Performed concurrently or sequentially

### Segmenting Contracts

Segment a single contract if:
- Separate proposals for each segment
- Separate negotiations
- Distinct components with separate pricing

## Contract Asset and Liability Presentation

### Balance Sheet

**Contract Assets:**
- Costs incurred plus recognized profits
- Less progress billings
- If positive: Asset

**Contract Liabilities:**
- Progress billings exceed recognized revenue
- Deferred revenue (unearned)

### Example Presentation

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Costs incurred | $3,000,000 |
| Recognized gross profit | $500,000 |
| Less: Progress billings | $(2,800,000) |
| **Contract Asset** | **$700,000** |

## Disclosure Requirements

### ASC 606 Contract Disclosures

1. **Revenue disaggregation** by type, geography, timing
2. **Contract balances** — assets and liabilities
3. **Performance obligations** — description and timing
4. **Significant judgments** — estimates, changes
5. **Contract costs** — capitalized amounts

### Construction-Specific Disclosures

1. Methods of measuring progress
2. Backlog of uncompleted contracts
3. Claims and change orders
4. Loss contract provisions

### Example Disclosure

> **Revenue Recognition:** The Company recognizes revenue from construction contracts over time using the cost-to-cost method to measure progress. Under this method, revenue is recognized in proportion to actual costs incurred relative to total estimated costs. Contract modifications are included in the transaction price when approved by the customer or when approval is probable.
>
> The Company had contract assets of $X million and contract liabilities of $X million as of December 31, 20XX. Contract assets represent revenue recognized in excess of billings, while contract liabilities represent billings in excess of revenue recognized.

## Private Company Considerations

### Common Issues

- Smaller contracts, simpler estimates
- Related party subcontracting
- Owner-managed projects
- Cash basis record keeping (conversion needed)

### Practical Approaches

1. Develop robust estimate process
2. Document percentage-of-completion calculations
3. Monitor for loss contracts
4. Track change orders and claims

---

## Common Audit Issues

1. **Estimate accuracy** — Cost-to-complete underestimated
2. **Loss recognition** — Delayed recognition of losses
3. **Change orders** — Recognized before approval
4. **Claims** — Overly aggressive recognition
5. **Percentage calculation** — Inconsistent methods
6. **Cutoff** — Costs in wrong period
7. **Related parties** — Subcontractor relationships
8. **Backlog** — Incomplete or inaccurate

## Comparison: Pre and Post ASC 606

| Aspect | Legacy (ASC 605-35) | Current (ASC 606) |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Methods | Percentage-of-completion, Completed contract | Cost-to-cost (primarily) |
| Criteria | Different for each method | Uniform over-time criteria |
| Losses | Immediate recognition | Same |
| Modifications | Various approaches | Specific guidance |
