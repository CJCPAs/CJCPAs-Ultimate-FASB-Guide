# ASC 970: Real Estate—General

> Specialized accounting guidance for real estate development, sales, and operations.

## Overview

ASC 970 provides accounting guidance for real estate transactions including sales, development costs, and rental operations. Real estate accounting has unique considerations for revenue recognition, cost capitalization, and impairment that differ from general GAAP.

**Core Principle:**
> "Real estate transactions require careful analysis of whether control has transferred, costs should be capitalized or expensed, and revenue recognition criteria are met."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Real estate sales

- Rental operations

## Real Estate Sales—ASC 606 Impact

### General Principle

Real estate sales follow ASC 606 revenue recognition:
- Identify contract
- Identify performance obligations
- Determine transaction price
- Allocate transaction price
- Recognize revenue when control transfers

### Control Transfer Analysis

| Indicator | Analysis |
|-----------|----------|
| **Legal title** | Has title passed? |
| **Physical possession** | Does buyer have access? |
| **Risks and rewards** | Who bears appreciation/depreciation risk? |
| **Right to payment** | Is payment assured? |
| **Customer acceptance** | Has buyer accepted? |

### Point in Time vs. Over Time

| Sale Type | Recognition |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Completed property** | Point in time (closing) |
| **Property under construction** | Analyze control transfer |
| **Continuing involvement** | May defer recognition |

## Types of Real Estate Sales

### Outright Sales

**Standard Sale—Control Transfers at Closing:**
```
Dr. Cash/Receivable              $1,000,000
    Cr. Revenue                           $1,000,000

Dr. Cost of Real Estate Sold       $700,000
    Cr. Real Estate Inventory             $700,000
```

### Sales with Continuing Involvement

| Involvement Type | Accounting Impact |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Seller guarantees** | May defer revenue |
| **Profit participation** | Allocate transaction price |
| **Management agreement** | Separate performance obligation |
| **Repurchase options** | Analyze for financing |

### Installment Sales

Under ASC 606:
- Recognize revenue at control transfer
- Consider financing component
- Assess collectibility

## Real Estate Development Costs

### Costs to Capitalize

| Cost Type | Capitalize? |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Land acquisition** | Yes |
| **Direct construction** | Yes |
| **Interest (qualifying)** | Yes (ASC 835) |
| **Property taxes during development** | Yes |
| **Insurance during development** | Yes |
| **Direct project management** | Yes |
| **Amenities** | Yes (allocated) |

### Costs to Expense

| Cost Type | Expense? |
|-----------|----------|
| **General and administrative** | Yes |
| **Selling costs** | Generally yes |
| **Abnormal costs (waste, inefficiency)** | Yes |
| **Idle facility costs** | Yes |

## Cost Capitalization—Journal Entries

### Land Acquisition

```
Dr. Land                         $2,000,000
Dr. Development Costs              $100,000
    Cr. Cash                              $2,100,000
(Land and closing costs)
```

### Construction Costs

```
Dr. Construction in Progress     $5,000,000
    Cr. Cash/Payables                    $5,000,000
```

### Capitalized Interest

```
Dr. Construction in Progress       $200,000
    Cr. Interest Expense                   $200,000
(Or credit Interest Payable)
```

### Transfer to Inventory

```
Dr. Real Estate Inventory        $7,300,000
    Cr. Land                              $2,000,000
    Cr. Development Costs                   $100,000
    Cr. Construction in Progress          $5,000,000
    Cr. Capitalized Interest                $200,000
```

## Pre-Acquisition Costs

### Capitalize If:

1. Costs are directly identifiable with property
2. Acquisition is probable
3. Expenditure occurs after acquisition is probable

### Expense If:

1. Acquisition is not probable
2. Costs are general or not directly identifiable
3. Property is abandoned

### Example

**Pre-Acquisition Costs (Probable):**
```
Dr. Pre-Acquisition Costs          $50,000
    Cr. Cash                                $50,000
(Survey, environmental studies for probable acquisition)
```

**If Acquisition Abandoned:**
```
Dr. Pre-Acquisition Expense        $50,000
    Cr. Pre-Acquisition Costs              $50,000
```

## Common Area and Amenities

### Cost Allocation

Amenity costs allocated to units sold:

```
Amenity Cost per Unit = Total Amenity Costs / Total Units
```

### Journal Entry

```
Dr. Cost of Real Estate Sold        $25,000
    Cr. Amenities Cost Pool                $25,000
(Allocation upon unit sale)
```

### Acquisition

```

```

```
Dr. Rent Receivable                $10,000

(Monthly rent recognition)
```

### Operating Expenses

```
Dr. Property Operating Expense     $3,000
    Cr. Cash/Payables                      $3,000
(Utilities, maintenance, etc.)
```

### Depreciation

```
Dr. Depreciation Expense          $12,500
    Cr. Accumulated Depreciation          $12,500
(Monthly depreciation of building)
```

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Condominium Development

**Facts:**
- Land cost: $3,000,000
- Construction costs: $15,000,000
- Capitalized interest: $800,000
- Amenities: $2,000,000
- Total units: 100
- Units sold in Year 1: 60

**Total Project Costs:**
```
Land                    $  3,000,000
Construction              15,000,000
Interest                     800,000
Amenities                  2,000,000
                        ------------
Total                   $ 20,800,000

Cost per unit: $208,000
```

**Revenue Recognition (60 units at $350,000 each):**
```
Dr. Cash/Receivable             $21,000,000
    Cr. Sales Revenue                     $21,000,000

Dr. Cost of Real Estate Sold    $12,480,000
    Cr. Real Estate Inventory            $12,480,000
(60 units × $208,000)
```

---

### Example 2: Land Sale with Note

**Facts:**
- Sale price: $500,000
- Down payment: $100,000
- Note receivable: $400,000 (5 years, 6% interest)
- Land cost: $300,000
- Market interest rate: 8%

**Analysis:**
- Significant financing component exists
- Must adjust transaction price

**Sale Entry:**
```
Dr. Cash                          $100,000
Dr. Note Receivable               $368,186
    Cr. Sales Revenue                       $468,186
(PV of note at market rate plus cash)

Dr. Cost of Real Estate Sold      $300,000
    Cr. Land Inventory                      $300,000
```

---

### Example 3: Sale-Leaseback

**Facts:**
- Sell building for $2,000,000
- Carrying value: $1,500,000
- Lease back for 5 years (operating lease)
- Sale qualifies as sale under ASC 606

**Sale Entry:**
```
Dr. Cash                        $2,000,000
    Cr. Building                          $1,500,000
    Cr. Gain on Sale                        $500,000
```

**Lease Entry (at commencement):**
```
Dr. Right-of-Use Asset            $XXX,XXX
    Cr. Lease Liability                   $XXX,XXX
```

## Time-Share Accounting

### Revenue Recognition

Time-share sales follow ASC 606 with additional guidance:

| Element | Treatment |
|---------|-----------|
| **Revenue** | When control transfers |
| **Financing** | Significant financing analysis |
| **Incentives** | Reduce transaction price |
| **Upgrades** | Separate if distinct |

### Cost Allocation

Costs allocated based on relative sales value:

```
Unit Cost Allocation = Total Costs × (Unit Value / Total Value)
```

## Impairment Considerations

### Real Estate Held for Development

| Type | Impairment Test |
|------|-----------------|
| **Inventory** | Lower of cost or NRV |
| **Assets to be held and used** | Recoverability then fair value |
| **Assets held for sale** | Lower of cost or FV less costs to sell |

### Indicators of Impairment

- Decline in market values
- Increased vacancy rates
- Reduced rental rates
- Construction delays
- Cost overruns
