# ASC 905: Agriculture

> Specialized accounting for agricultural producers, including crops, livestock, and cooperatives.

## Overview

ASC 905 provides industry-specific guidance for agricultural producers, covering the unique accounting issues related to growing crops, raising livestock, and operating agricultural cooperatives. Agriculture presents distinct challenges due to biological transformation, harvest cycles, and commodity pricing.

> **Core Principle:** Agricultural activities involve biological transformation of living assets, requiring specialized approaches to inventory measurement, cost allocation, and revenue recognition.

### Applies To

- Farmers and ranchers
- Agricultural producers

- Entities with farming operations

### Key Agricultural Assets

| Category | Examples |

## Inventory and Cost Accounting

### Growing Crops

**Cost accumulation includes:**
- Seed and planting costs
- Fertilizer and chemicals
- Labor (planting, cultivation, harvest)
- Irrigation and water
- Equipment costs (allocated)
- Land preparation

### Measurement Options

| Method | Application |
|--------|-------------|
| **Cost accumulation** | Track actual costs until harvest |
| **Lower of cost or NRV** | After harvest |
| **Market value** | For immediate or near-term sale commodities |

### Pre-Harvest Costs

**Capitalize costs incurred:**
```
Dr. Growing Crops (Inventory)             $100,000
    Cr. Cash/Accounts Payable                   $100,000
```

### At Harvest

**Transfer to harvested inventory:**
```
Dr. Grain Inventory                       $150,000
    Cr. Growing Crops                           $150,000
```

## Livestock Accounting

### Types of Livestock

| Category | Characteristics | Accounting |
|----------|-----------------|------------|
| **Raised for sale** | Feeder cattle, market hogs | Inventory |
| **Held for breeding** | Bulls, brood sows | Fixed asset |
| **Dairy/Production** | Dairy cows, laying hens | Depreciable asset |

### Raised Livestock (Inventory)

**Cost components:**
- Purchase price or birth value
- Feed costs
- Veterinary expenses
- Labor
- Allocated overhead

**Journal Entry—Purchase feeder cattle:**
```
Dr. Livestock Inventory                   $50,000
    Cr. Cash                                     $50,000
```

### Breeding Livestock (Fixed Asset)

**Capitalize and depreciate:**
```
Dr. Breeding Livestock                    $25,000
    Cr. Cash                                     $25,000
```

**Annual Depreciation:**
```
Dr. Depreciation Expense                  $5,000
    Cr. Accumulated Depreciation                 $5,000
```

### Livestock Births

**Raised offspring—assign reasonable cost:**
```
Dr. Livestock Inventory (calf)            $200
    Cr. Cost of Production                       $200
```

## Development Costs

### Orchards, Groves, and Vineyards

**Pre-productive period costs:**
- Land preparation
- Planting costs
- Cultivation until mature
- Interest (may capitalize per ASC 835)

**Treatment:** Capitalize until plants are productive

### Example—Vineyard Development

| Year | Activity | Treatment |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Year 1 | Land prep, planting | Capitalize |
| Year 2-3 | Cultivation, no harvest | Capitalize |
| Year 4+ | Productive | Depreciate; expense ongoing costs |

**Journal Entry—Development Costs:**
```
Dr. Vineyard Development                 $200,000
    Cr. Cash                                    $200,000
```

**When Productive—Begin Depreciation:**
```
Dr. Depreciation Expense                 $10,000
    Cr. Accumulated Depreciation                $10,000
```

## Revenue Recognition

### General Principles

Agricultural revenue follows ASC 606 principles with industry considerations:

| Transaction | Recognition |
|-------------|-------------|
| Commodity sales | At delivery/transfer of control |
| Forward contracts | Per contract terms |
| Government payments | When earned and realizable |
| Cooperative patronage | When determinable |

### Commodity Sales

**Sale at delivery:**
```
Dr. Accounts Receivable                  $100,000
    Cr. Revenue—Grain Sales                     $100,000

Dr. Cost of Goods Sold                   $75,000
    Cr. Inventory—Grain                         $75,000
```

### Government Payments

**Types:**
- Direct payments
- Price support
- Conservation payments
- Disaster assistance

**Recognition:** When conditions are met and amounts are determinable

```
Dr. Government Payment Receivable         $25,000
    Cr. Government Payment Revenue              $25,000
```

## Agricultural Cooperatives

### Unique Characteristics

- Owned by member-producers
- Operate at cost (or near cost)
- Distribute patronage to members
- May have non-member business

### Patronage Dividends

**Allocation to members based on:**
- Volume of business
- Value of products marketed
- Purchases from cooperative

**Journal Entry—Patronage Allocation:**
```
Dr. Patronage Dividend Expense           $500,000
    Cr. Patronage Payable (cash portion)        $100,000
    Cr. Member Equity (retained portion)        $400,000
```

### Per-Unit Retains

Amounts retained by cooperative from member sales:
- Build cooperative equity
- Eventually distributed to members

## Fair Value Considerations

### Agricultural Commodities

Commodities with **quoted market prices** may be measured at fair value less costs to sell:

**Conditions:**
- Reliable, quoted prices
- No significant processing required
- Immediate marketability

**When appropriate:**
```
Dr. Inventory                            $5,000
    Cr. Gain on Agricultural Commodities        $5,000
```
*(Adjust to market value)*

### Biological Assets (IFRS Comparison)

| Topic | US GAAP | IFRS (IAS 41) |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Growing crops | Cost or NRV | Fair value |
| Livestock | Cost or market | Fair value |
| Bearer plants | Cost, depreciate | Cost, depreciate |

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Crop Farming Cycle

**Spring—Planting:**
```
Dr. Growing Crops                        $50,000
    Cr. Cash (seed, fertilizer)                 $50,000
```

**Summer—Cultivation:**
```
Dr. Growing Crops                        $30,000
    Cr. Cash (labor, chemicals)                 $30,000
```

**Fall—Harvest:**
```
Dr. Grain Inventory                      $80,000
    Cr. Growing Crops                           $80,000
```

**Sale:**
```
Dr. Cash                                $120,000
    Cr. Grain Sales                            $120,000

Dr. Cost of Sales                        $80,000
    Cr. Grain Inventory                         $80,000
```

### Example 2: Cattle Ranching

**Purchase feeder cattle:**
```
Dr. Livestock Inventory                 $200,000
    Cr. Cash                                   $200,000
```

**Feed and care costs:**
```
Dr. Livestock Inventory                  $75,000
    Cr. Cash/Accounts Payable                   $75,000
```

**Sale of fattened cattle:**
```
Dr. Cash                                $350,000
    Cr. Livestock Sales                        $350,000

Dr. Cost of Livestock Sold              $275,000
    Cr. Livestock Inventory                    $275,000
```

## Income Tax Considerations

### Cash vs. Accrual Method

| Method | Characteristics |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Cash** | Common for farmers; revenue when received |
| **Accrual** | Required for certain large farms |

### GAAP vs. Tax Differences

| Item | GAAP | Tax |
|------|------|-----|
| Growing crops | Capitalize | May deduct currently |
| Prepaid expenses | Defer | May deduct (with limits) |
| Livestock | Capitalize | Various elections |

Creates deferred tax considerations for financial reporting.

## Disclosure Requirements

### Industry-Specific Disclosures

1. **Inventory methods** used
2. **Growing crops** valuation method
3. **Livestock** classification and valuation
4. **Government programs** participation
5. **Weather/market risks** affecting operations

### Example Disclosure

> **Agriculture Inventory:** Crops in the ground are stated at accumulated cost of production. Harvested grain inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Livestock held for sale is valued at the lower of cost or market, while breeding livestock is stated at cost less accumulated depreciation.

## Private Company Considerations

### Practical Issues

- Most farms are privately held
- Often use cash basis for tax
- May have informal accounting systems
- Family operations with related parties

### Common Challenges

1. Inventory cost allocation
2. Land valuation (often understated)
3. Equipment depreciation
4. Related party transactions

---

## Common Audit Issues

1. **Inventory existence** — Physical counts at remote locations
2. **Valuation** — Market prices, obsolescence
3. **Cutoff** — Harvest timing, storage
4. **Cost allocation** — Joint costs among crops
5. **Government payments** — Proper recognition timing
6. **Livestock counts** — Verification challenges
7. **Related parties** — Land leases, labor from owners
