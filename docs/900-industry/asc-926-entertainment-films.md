# ASC 926: Entertainment—Films

> Specialized accounting for motion picture production, distribution, and revenue participation.

## Overview

ASC 926 provides industry-specific guidance for the film industry, addressing the unique accounting challenges of motion picture production, acquisition, distribution, and revenue participation arrangements.

> **Core Principle:** Film production costs are capitalized and amortized using the individual-film-forecast method, matching costs to the revenue generated over the film's life cycle.

### Applies To

## Film Cost Capitalization

### Capitalizable Costs

| Capitalize | Expense |
|------------|---------|
| Direct production costs | General & administrative |
| Production overhead | Marketing and advertising |
| Interest (during production) | Unsuccessful project costs |
| Acquired screenplay costs | Development costs (until greenlit) |

### Production Phases

| Phase | Costs | Treatment |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| **Development** | Scripts, option fees | Expense until greenlit |
| **Pre-production** | Casting, location scouting | Capitalize when greenlit |
| **Production** | Filming, talent, crew | Capitalize |
| **Post-production** | Editing, effects, sound | Capitalize |

### Journal Entry—Production Costs

```
Dr. Film Costs—Production              $50,000,000
    Cr. Cash/Accounts Payable                 $50,000,000
```

## Film Cost Amortization

### Individual-Film-Forecast Method

**Formula:**
```
Period Amortization = (Current Period Revenue / Estimated Total Revenue) × Unamortized Film Cost
```

### Revenue Forecast Period

**Maximum forecast period:** 10 years from initial release

**Must include:**
- Theatrical revenue
- Home entertainment
- Television licensing
- Streaming/digital
- Merchandise (if significant)

### Example Calculation

**Film Costs:** $100,000,000
**Total Estimated Revenue:** $250,000,000

**Year 1 Revenue:** $150,000,000

**Year 1 Amortization:**
```
($150,000,000 / $250,000,000) × $100,000,000 = $60,000,000
```

**Journal Entry:**
```
Dr. Film Cost Amortization             $60,000,000
    Cr. Accumulated Amortization              $60,000,000
```

## Participation Costs

### Types of Participations

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Gross participation** | Based on gross revenue |
| **Net participation** | Based on net profit |
| **Residuals** | Union-mandated payments |
| **Bonuses** | Performance-based payments |

### Accounting Treatment

**Expense participation costs in same ratio as film costs:**

```
Period Participation = (Current Period Revenue / Total Estimated Revenue) × Total Estimated Participations
```

### Journal Entry

```
Dr. Participation Expense               $5,000,000
    Cr. Participation Liability                 $5,000,000
```

### Residuals

**Union residuals:** Expense when film is exhibited in applicable medium

```
Dr. Residual Expense                   $1,000,000
    Cr. Residuals Payable                      $1,000,000
```

## Revenue Recognition

### License Agreements

**Recognize when ALL criteria met:**
1. License period has begun
2. Customer can exploit the film
3. Fee is fixed or determinable
4. Collection is reasonably assured

### Theatrical Revenue

**Recognize when:**
- Film is exhibited
- Box office receipts determined
- Distribution fee calculated

```
Dr. Accounts Receivable                $20,000,000
    Cr. Theatrical Revenue                    $20,000,000
```

### Home Entertainment

**Physical media (declining):**
- Recognize when shipped to retailers
- Reserve for returns

**Digital/Streaming:**
- Recognize over license period
- Or per transaction (EST/VOD)

### Television/Streaming Licenses

**Fixed fee licenses:**
```
Dr. Accounts Receivable (or Receivable)
    Cr. Licensing Revenue
```
*When license period begins and film available*

**Variable fee (per-subscriber):**
- Recognize as reported by licensee
- Estimate if not yet reported

## Impairment Testing

### When to Test

Test for impairment when events indicate carrying amount may not be recovered:
- Poor box office performance
- Cancelled distribution agreements
- Market conditions change

### Impairment Measurement

**Impairment Loss = Carrying Amount − Fair Value**

**Fair Value typically based on:**
- Discounted future cash flows
- Comparable transactions
- Market approach

### Journal Entry

```
Dr. Impairment Loss—Film Costs        $15,000,000
    Cr. Film Costs                           $15,000,000
```

### Ultimate Revenue Changes

**Periodically reassess ultimate revenue:**
- At least quarterly
- Adjust remaining amortization prospectively
- Test for impairment if significant decline

## Episodic Television Content

### Cost Capitalization

Similar to films, but:
- Capitalize pilot costs
- Subsequent episodes—capitalize and amortize
- Series commitment considerations

### Amortization

**Options:**
| Method | Application |
|--------|-------------|
| **Episode** | Per-episode broadcast |
| **Season** | Over season exhibition |
| **Series** | Over series life |

### Example—TV Series

**Season production cost:** $20,000,000 (10 episodes)
**Estimated revenue:** $30,000,000

**Per episode airdate:**
```
Episode amortization = ($3,000,000 / $30,000,000) × $20,000,000 = $2,000,000
```

## Development Costs

### Treatment Before Greenlight

**Expense as incurred:**
- Script development
- Option payments
- Early pre-production

### Set-Off of Development Costs

If project is greenlit, previously expensed costs are **not** reinstated.

### Abandoned Projects

```
Dr. Development Expense (write-off)    $500,000
    Cr. Development Costs Asset               $500,000
```

## Co-Productions and Joint Ventures

### Accounting Approaches

| Arrangement | Treatment |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Joint ownership** | Each party records share of costs/revenue |
| **Distribution agreement** | Producer capitalizes; distributor has rights |
| **Co-financing** | Allocate based on rights obtained |

### Third-Party Contributions

**Reduce film cost basis by:**
- Advance payments from licensees
- Government grants/subsidies
- Tax credits (may vary by jurisdiction)

```
Dr. Cash (tax credit)                   $5,000,000
    Cr. Film Costs                             $5,000,000
```

## Disclosure Requirements

### Required Disclosures

1. **Accounting policies** for film costs
2. **Amortization method** and rates
3. **Components of film costs** by category
4. **Events or changes** affecting ultimate revenue
5. **Participation accruals** and methodology

### Example Disclosure

> **Film Costs:** Film costs include direct production costs, production overhead, and interest. These costs are capitalized and amortized using the individual-film-forecast method, whereby costs are amortized in the ratio that current period revenue bears to estimated ultimate revenue. Ultimate revenue estimates are reviewed quarterly and adjusted as necessary.

## Streaming and Digital Evolution

### Modern Considerations

| Topic | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|
| **Exclusive streaming content** | Capitalize, amortize over license |
| **Multi-platform release** | Allocate costs based on relative value |
| **Direct-to-streaming** | Same capitalization principles |
| **Interactive content** | Emerging—apply judgment |

### Content Libraries

**Acquired libraries:**
- Record at fair value
- Amortize based on expected revenue pattern

## Common Audit Issues

1. **Ultimate revenue estimates** — Overly optimistic projections
2. **Impairment timing** — Delayed recognition
3. **Participation calculations** — Complex formulas
4. **Capitalization criteria** — Costs included inappropriately
5. **Revenue recognition** — Timing of license revenue
6. **Greenlight determination** — Development vs. production
7. **Fair value measurement** — Support for impairment tests
