## Key Standards Summary

### ASC 205: Presentation of Financial Statements

**Going Concern (ASC 205-40)**

Management must evaluate whether substantial doubt exists about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern.

**Plain English:** Can your company survive the next 12 months? If there's significant doubt, you need to disclose it.

**Evaluation Period:** 12 months from financial statement issuance date

**Key Disclosures:**
- Principal conditions raising doubt
- Management's plans to address the conditions
- Whether plans are likely to be effective

### ASC 230: Statement of Cash Flows

📌 **Major Standard — Critical financial statement preparation**

See detailed guide: [ASC 230: Statement of Cash Flows](asc-230-cash-flows.md)

**Classification of Cash Flows:**

| Activity | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Operating** | Cash from customers, payments to suppliers, interest paid/received |
| **Investing** | Purchase/sale of equipment, investments, loans to others |
| **Financing** | Borrowings, debt repayments, equity issuances, dividends |

**Methods:**
- **Direct Method** — Shows actual cash receipts and payments (preferred)
- **Indirect Method** — Reconciles net income to operating cash flows (most common)

## Practical Tips

### Balance Sheet Classification

**Current vs. Noncurrent:**
- Current = expected to be realized, sold, or consumed within normal operating cycle (or 12 months if no operating cycle)
- Consider the nature of the item, not just timing

### Cash Flow Statement

**Common Mistakes:**
1. Misclassifying interest paid (operating, not financing)
2. Incorrectly treating principal payments on finance leases (financing)
3. Netting receipts and payments when gross presentation is required

## Private Company Considerations

Private companies can elect to:
- Not disclose segment information (unless otherwise required by contract)
- Use simplified interim reporting in some circumstances
