# ASC 210: Balance Sheet

> The foundational standard for presenting an entity's financial position at a point in time.

## Overview

ASC 210 provides guidance on the classification and presentation of items on the balance sheet (statement of financial position). It establishes the framework for distinguishing between current and noncurrent assets and liabilities, and addresses specific presentation matters including offsetting, classification, and valuation allowances.

**Core Principle:**
> "A classified balance sheet distinguishes between current and noncurrent assets and liabilities, providing useful information about an entity's liquidity and financial flexibility."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- All entities presenting a balance sheet in conformity with U.S. GAAP
- Classification of assets and liabilities
- Offsetting and netting presentations
- Valuation allowance presentation

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Specific measurement guidance (covered by individual ASC topics)
- Entities using unclassified balance sheets (certain industries)
- Statement of cash flows presentation (ASC 230)

## Current vs. Noncurrent Classification

### Current Assets

**Definition:** Assets reasonably expected to be realized in cash, sold, or consumed within one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer.

| Type | Examples | Classification Criteria |
|------|----------|------------------------|
| **Cash and equivalents** | Bank accounts, money markets, T-bills < 90 days | Unrestricted and available |
| **Short-term investments** | Trading securities, AFS maturing < 1 year | Intent and ability to liquidate |
| **Receivables** | Trade A/R, notes receivable | Expected collection within cycle |
| **Inventory** | Raw materials, WIP, finished goods | Normal operating cycle |
| **Prepaid expenses** | Insurance, rent, supplies | Benefit received within year |

### Noncurrent Assets

**Definition:** Assets not meeting current asset criteria.

| Type | Examples | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Property, plant, and equipment** | Land, buildings, machinery | Net of accumulated depreciation |
| **Intangible assets** | Goodwill, patents, trademarks | Net of accumulated amortization |
| **Long-term investments** | HTM securities, equity method | Intent to hold beyond one year |
| **Other assets** | Deferred charges, restricted cash | Various long-term items |

### Current Liabilities

**Definition:** Obligations expected to be liquidated through use of current assets or creation of other current liabilities within one year or operating cycle.

| Type | Examples | Classification Criteria |
|------|----------|------------------------|
| **Accounts payable** | Trade payables | Due within normal terms |
| **Short-term debt** | Lines of credit, current portion LTD | Due within one year |
| **Accrued expenses** | Wages, interest, taxes | Incurred but not yet paid |
| **Deferred revenue** | Customer deposits, prepayments | Expected to be earned within year |
| **Current portion of leases** | Finance and operating lease liabilities | Payments due within year |

### Noncurrent Liabilities

**Definition:** Obligations not meeting current liability criteria.

| Type | Examples | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Long-term debt** | Bonds, term loans, mortgages | Excluding current portion |
| **Lease liabilities** | Finance and operating leases | Excluding current portion |
| **Deferred tax liabilities** | Temporary differences | Generally noncurrent |
| **Pension obligations** | Defined benefit liability | Net of plan assets |
| **Other liabilities** | Asset retirement obligations | Long-term obligations |

## Operating Cycle Concept

### Definition

The **operating cycle** is the average time between:
1. Acquisition of materials/services entering the process, and
2. Final cash realization from sales of products/services

### Application

| Situation | Classification Period |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Operating cycle < 1 year | Use one-year period |
| Operating cycle > 1 year | Use operating cycle |
| No clearly defined cycle | Use one-year period |

### Examples

| Industry | Typical Operating Cycle |
|----------|------------------------|
| Retail | 3-6 months |
| Manufacturing | 6-12 months |
| Construction | 12-36 months |
| Distillery (aging products) | 3-15 years |

**Plain English:** If your business takes longer than a year to turn inventory into cash (like a whiskey distillery aging barrels), you can use that longer cycle for current/noncurrent classification.

## Offsetting (Netting) Assets and Liabilities

### General Rule

**Offsetting is prohibited** unless specifically permitted by GAAP.

### When Offsetting Is Permitted

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Right of setoff exists** | Legal right to set off recognized amounts |
| **Intent to offset** | Intent to settle net or simultaneously |
| **Same counterparty** | Amounts owed to and from same party |

### Master Netting Arrangements (ASC 210-20)

For derivative instruments and repurchase agreements:

| Situation | Presentation |
|-----------|--------------|
| Right of setoff + intent to net | May present net |
| Right of setoff but no intent | Present gross with disclosure |
| No right of setoff | Must present gross |

### Common Offsetting Scenarios

| Scenario | Permitted? | Rationale |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| A/R and A/P with same customer | Only if right of setoff exists | Separate legal claims |
| Cash in bank vs. overdraft (same bank) | Yes, if same account | Legal right exists |
| Cash vs. overdraft (different banks) | No | No right of setoff |
| Deferred tax assets/liabilities | Yes, same jurisdiction | ASC 740 permits |

## Debt Classification

### Short-Term Debt Expected to Be Refinanced

**Issue:** When can short-term debt be classified as long-term?

### Classification as Noncurrent (ASC 470-10-45)

Short-term obligations may be classified as noncurrent if:

| Method | Requirements |
|--------|--------------|
| **Actual refinancing** | Refinanced on long-term basis AFTER balance sheet date but BEFORE financial statements issued |
| **Intent + Ability** | Intent to refinance AND ability demonstrated by financing agreement |

### Ability to Refinance—Financing Agreement Requirements

The agreement must:
1. Be with a lender (or equity investors) capable of providing financing
2. Not expire within one year (or operating cycle)
3. Not be cancelable by lender (except violation terms)
4. Not require repayment before classification period ends

### Debt Covenant Violations

| Situation | Classification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Violation at balance sheet date | Current (unless waiver obtained) |
| Violation waived (short-term) | Current |
| Violation waived (> 1 year) | May be noncurrent |
| Probable future violation | Consider disclosure, not automatic reclassification |

## Valuation Allowances

### Presentation

| Account | Presentation |
|---------|--------------|
| **Allowance for credit losses** | Deducted from receivables (contra-asset) |
| **Accumulated depreciation** | Deducted from PP&E (contra-asset) |
| **Accumulated amortization** | Deducted from intangibles (contra-asset) |
| **Inventory valuation reserve** | Deducted from inventory (contra-asset) |
| **Deferred tax valuation allowance** | Deducted from DTA (contra-asset) |

### Disclosure

Valuation allowances should be:
- Deducted from related asset on face of balance sheet, OR
- Shown parenthetically with gross amount and allowance, OR
- Disclosed in notes with reconciliation

## Classified vs. Unclassified Balance Sheets

### Classified Balance Sheet

Standard presentation with current/noncurrent distinction:
- Required for most entities
- Provides liquidity information
- Common format for commercial enterprises

### Unclassified Balance Sheet

No current/noncurrent distinction:

| Industry/Entity | Reason |
|-----------------|--------|
| Investment companies | Assets measured at fair value |
| Broker-dealers | Different presentation requirements |
| Certain financial institutions | Liquidity sequence more relevant |

---

## Order of Presentation

### Assets

**Typical Order (Liquidity Sequence):**
1. Cash and cash equivalents
2. Short-term investments
3. Accounts receivable (net)
4. Inventory
5. Prepaid expenses
6. Other current assets
7. Property, plant, and equipment (net)
8. Intangible assets
9. Other noncurrent assets

### Liabilities and Equity

**Typical Order:**
1. Accounts payable
2. Accrued expenses
3. Short-term debt
4. Current portion of long-term debt
5. Other current liabilities
6. Long-term debt
7. Other noncurrent liabilities
8. Stockholders' equity

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Debt Classification with Refinancing

**Facts:**
- $1,000,000 line of credit due March 15, Year 2
- Balance sheet date: December 31, Year 1
- January 15, Year 2: Company signs 5-year term loan to refinance
- Financial statements issued: February 28, Year 2

**Analysis:**
- Short-term debt refinanced before statements issued
- Classify as noncurrent

**Balance Sheet Presentation (December 31, Year 1):**
```
Long-term debt:
  Term loan refinancing line of credit    $1,000,000
```

**Disclosure Required:**
"In January Year 2, the Company refinanced its $1,000,000 line of credit with a five-year term loan bearing interest at X%."

---

### Example 2: Offsetting Analysis

**Facts:**
- Company owes Vendor A: $50,000 (accounts payable)
- Vendor A owes Company: $20,000 (accounts receivable)
- No contractual right of setoff exists

**Analysis:**
- No legal right of setoff = No offsetting permitted

**Correct Presentation:**
```
Assets:
  Accounts receivable                     $20,000

Liabilities:
  Accounts payable                        $50,000
```

**Incorrect (Not Permitted):**
```
Liabilities:
  Accounts payable (net)                  $30,000
```

---

### Example 3: Operating Cycle Classification

**Facts:**
- Whiskey distillery with 5-year aging process
- Inventory at December 31, Year 1:
  - Barrels aged 1 year: $500,000
  - Barrels aged 3 years: $750,000
  - Barrels aged 5 years (ready for sale): $1,200,000

**Analysis:**
- Operating cycle is 5 years
- ALL inventory is current (within operating cycle)

**Balance Sheet:**
```
Current assets:
  Inventory (including whiskey aging
    up to 5 years)                        $2,450,000
```

---

### Example 4: Debt Covenant Violation

**Facts:**
- $5,000,000 term loan due Year 5
- Debt covenant requires current ratio > 1.5
- At December 31, Year 1: Current ratio = 1.3 (violation)
- Lender provides waiver through March 31, Year 2

**Analysis:**
- Waiver is less than one year
- Debt is callable (lender can accelerate after waiver expires)
- Classify as current

**Balance Sheet:**
```
Current liabilities:
  Long-term debt, classified as current
    due to covenant violation              $5,000,000
```

## Private Company Considerations

### Simplified Classification

Private companies may use less detailed classifications:
- Fewer line items acceptable
- Combined categories may be appropriate
- Should still distinguish current from noncurrent

### Bank Reporting Requirements

Many private companies have lender requirements:
- Specific line items may be required
- Working capital calculations affect covenants
- Discuss with lender before simplification

### Common Private Company Issues

| Issue | Consideration |
|-------|---------------|
| Related party receivables | Evaluate collectibility; may need separate line |
| Shareholder loans | Classify based on expected repayment |
| Intercompany balances | Eliminate in consolidation or disclose |

## Common Audit Issues

### Classification Errors

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| **Current portion of long-term debt** | Understated current liabilities, overstated working capital |
| **Restricted cash** | Overstated current assets if restricted > 1 year |
| **Receivables from affiliates** | May not be current if collection uncertain |
| **Debt covenant violations** | Potential current classification of entire debt |

### Offsetting Violations

| Error | Risk |
|-------|------|
| Netting A/R and A/P | Understates both assets and liabilities |
| Netting cash and overdrafts (different banks) | Misstates cash position |
| Netting intercompany balances | Reduces transparency |

### Documentation Requirements

- Working capital calculations
- Operating cycle determination (if > 1 year)
- Refinancing agreements and intent
- Covenant compliance/waiver letters
- Right of setoff documentation

## Disclosure Requirements

### General Requirements

| Item | Disclosure |
|------|------------|
| **Significant accounting policies** | Classification methods used |
| **Maturity information** | For significant liabilities |
| **Restricted assets** | Nature and amount of restrictions |
| **Pledged assets** | Assets pledged as collateral |

### Debt-Specific Disclosures

| Item | Disclosure |
|------|------------|
| **Refinancing arrangements** | Terms and conditions |
| **Covenant violations** | Nature, amounts, waiver terms |
| **Short-term debt classified as long-term** | Circumstances and terms |
