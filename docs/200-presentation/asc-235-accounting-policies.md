# ASC 235: Notes to Financial Statements

> Disclosure of significant accounting policies and the foundation of financial statement transparency.

## Overview

ASC 235 establishes the requirement to disclose significant accounting policies as an integral part of the financial statements. This disclosure enables users to better understand the basis on which financial statements are prepared and enhances comparability across entities.

**Core Principle:**
> "A description of all significant accounting policies of a reporting entity shall be included as an integral part of the financial statements."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- All entities preparing financial statements in conformity with GAAP
- Disclosure of accounting policies
- Changes in accounting policies

### Related Topics:
- Accounting changes (ASC 250)
- Significant estimates (ASC 275)
- Subsequent events (ASC 855)
- Fair value disclosures (ASC 820)

---

## Key Principles

### What Must Be Disclosed

Disclosure should identify and describe:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Selection from alternatives** | FIFO vs. LIFO, depreciation methods |
| **Industry-specific practices** | Revenue recognition for specialized industries |
| **Unusual applications** | Application of GAAP in unique situations |
| **Significant policies** | Those impacting financial position and results |

### What Should NOT Be Included

| Exclude | Reason |
|---------|--------|
| **Repetition of GAAP** | Readers assumed to understand GAAP |
| **Immaterial items** | Focus on significant policies |
| **Detailed calculations** | Belongs in specific note |
| **Predictions** | Accounting policies are historical |

## Presentation

### Location

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Summary note** | Separate "Summary of Significant Accounting Policies" |
| **First note** | Typically Note 1 to financial statements |
| **Initial section** | Before specific notes |

### Format

```
NOTE 1: SUMMARY OF SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES

Basis of Presentation
[Description of preparation basis]

Revenue Recognition
[Description of revenue policy]

Inventory
[Description of inventory method]

Property, Plant, and Equipment
[Description of depreciation policy]

[Additional policies...]
```

## Common Accounting Policy Disclosures

### 1. Basis of Presentation

```
Basis of Presentation

The consolidated financial statements include the accounts of the
Company and its wholly-owned subsidiaries. All significant
intercompany transactions and balances have been eliminated in
consolidation. The financial statements have been prepared in
accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the
United States of America (U.S. GAAP).
```

### 2. Use of Estimates

```
Use of Estimates

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with U.S.
GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that
affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosure
of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial
statements, and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses
during the reporting period. Significant estimates include the
allowance for credit losses, useful lives of long-lived assets,
valuation of goodwill and intangibles, accrued liabilities, and
income taxes. Actual results could differ from those estimates.
```

### 3. Revenue Recognition

```
Revenue Recognition

The Company recognizes revenue in accordance with ASC 606, Revenue
from Contracts with Customers. Revenue is recognized when control
of promised goods or services is transferred to customers, in an
amount that reflects the consideration the Company expects to be
entitled to receive in exchange for those goods or services.

Product revenue is recognized at the point in time when products
are shipped or delivered, depending on contract terms. Service
revenue is recognized over time as services are rendered.
```

### 4. Cash and Cash Equivalents

```
Cash and Cash Equivalents

The Company considers all highly liquid investments with original
maturities of three months or less at the date of purchase to be
cash equivalents. Cash equivalents consist primarily of money
market funds and short-term government securities.
```

### 5. Accounts Receivable and Credit Losses

```
Accounts Receivable and Allowance for Credit Losses

Accounts receivable are recorded at the invoiced amount and do
not bear interest. The Company maintains an allowance for credit
losses based on the expected collectibility of receivables. The
allowance is estimated using historical loss experience, current
conditions, and reasonable and supportable forecasts. Receivables
are written off when deemed uncollectible.
```

### 6. Inventory

```
Inventory

Inventory is stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value.
Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out (FIFO) method.
The Company periodically reviews inventory for excess, obsolete,
or slow-moving items and records valuation allowances as
appropriate.
```

### 7. Property, Plant, and Equipment

```
Property, Plant, and Equipment

Property, plant, and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated
depreciation. Depreciation is calculated using the straight-line
method over the estimated useful lives of the assets:

  Buildings and improvements        15-40 years
  Machinery and equipment            5-15 years
  Furniture and fixtures             5-10 years
  Computer equipment                  3-5 years

Leasehold improvements are amortized over the shorter of the
lease term or estimated useful life. Maintenance and repairs
are expensed as incurred.
```

### 8. Goodwill and Intangible Assets

```
Goodwill and Intangible Assets

Goodwill represents the excess of purchase price over the fair
value of net assets acquired in business combinations. Goodwill
is not amortized but is tested for impairment annually as of
October 1, or more frequently if indicators of impairment exist.

Intangible assets with finite lives are amortized over their
estimated useful lives. Intangible assets with indefinite lives
are tested for impairment annually. The Company evaluates the
remaining useful life of intangible assets each reporting period.
```

### 9. Leases

```
Leases

The Company determines if an arrangement is a lease at inception.
Right-of-use (ROU) assets and lease liabilities are recognized
for operating and finance leases with terms greater than 12 months.
The Company has elected the short-term lease exemption for leases
with terms of 12 months or less.

ROU assets represent the Company's right to use an underlying
asset, and lease liabilities represent the obligation to make
lease payments. These are recognized at lease commencement based
on the present value of lease payments over the lease term,
discounted using the Company's incremental borrowing rate.
```

### 10. Income Taxes

```
Income Taxes

Income taxes are accounted for using the asset and liability
method. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognized for
the future tax consequences attributable to differences between
the financial statement carrying amounts of existing assets and
liabilities and their respective tax bases. Deferred tax assets
are reduced by a valuation allowance when it is more likely than
not that some or all of the deferred tax assets will not be
realized. The Company recognizes the tax benefit from uncertain
tax positions only if it is more likely than not that the
position will be sustained.
```

## Industry-Specific Policies

### Construction Company

```
Revenue Recognition—Construction Contracts

Revenue from long-term construction contracts is recognized over
time using the input method based on costs incurred relative to
total estimated costs. Contract costs include all direct costs
and indirect costs allocable to contracts. Changes in estimates
are recognized in the period in which they are determined.
```

### Software Company

```
Software Development Costs

Costs incurred in the preliminary project stage and
post-implementation stage are expensed as incurred. Costs incurred
during the application development stage are capitalized and
amortized on a straight-line basis over the estimated useful life
of the software, typically 3-5 years.
```

### Investment Company

```
Investment Valuation

Investments are carried at fair value. Securities traded on
national securities exchanges are valued at the last reported
sales price. Securities for which market quotations are not
readily available are valued at fair value as determined in
good faith by the Board of Directors.
```

## Changes in Accounting Policies

### Required Disclosures

When accounting policy changes:

| Element | Disclosure |
|---------|------------|
| **Nature of change** | What changed |
| **Reason for change** | Why change made |
| **Method of adoption** | Retrospective or prospective |
| **Effect on financial statements** | Quantified impact |

### Example Disclosure

```
Change in Accounting Policy

Effective January 1, 20X1, the Company adopted ASU 20XX-XX,
[Name of Standard]. The standard requires [description]. The
Company adopted the standard using the modified retrospective
method. The cumulative effect of adoption was an increase to
retained earnings of $X million as of January 1, 20X1.
```

## Recently Adopted and Pending Standards

### Recently Adopted

```
Recently Adopted Accounting Standards

In [month/year], the Company adopted ASU 20XX-XX, [Name]. This
standard [description of standard]. The adoption [did/did not]
have a material impact on the Company's financial statements.
```

### Not Yet Adopted

```
Accounting Standards Not Yet Adopted

In [month/year], the FASB issued ASU 20XX-XX, [Name]. This
standard [description]. The standard is effective for fiscal
years beginning after [date]. The Company is currently evaluating
the impact of adoption on its financial statements.
```

## Practical Tips

### What Makes a Good Policy Disclosure

| Quality | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Entity-specific** | Tailored to the company's operations |
| **Clear** | Understandable to financial statement users |
| **Complete** | Covers all significant policies |
| **Concise** | Not unnecessarily lengthy |
| **Current** | Reflects current policies and standards |

### Common Deficiencies

| Deficiency | Improvement |
|------------|-------------|
| **Boilerplate language** | Customize to entity |
| **Outdated standards** | Update references |
| **Missing policies** | Add all significant items |
| **Too detailed** | Summarize appropriately |
| **Inconsistent** | Ensure consistency with F/S |

## Private Company Considerations

### Simplified Disclosures

Private companies may have:
- Fewer accounting policies
- Simpler arrangements
- Reduced disclosure requirements

### Common Private Company Policies

| Policy | Private Company Application |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Goodwill** | May elect to amortize |
| **Leases** | Risk-free rate election |
| **VIEs** | Simplified consolidation |
| **Hedging** | Simplified hedge accounting |

### Policy Disclosures

| Issue | Audit Focus |
|-------|-------------|
| **Completeness** | All significant policies included |

- Compare to prior year
- Verify policies match accounting
- Check for new standards requiring disclosure
