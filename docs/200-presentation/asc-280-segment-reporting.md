# ASC 280: Segment Reporting

> The comprehensive standard for disclosing information about operating segments in annual and interim financial reports.

## Overview

ASC 280 requires public entities to report financial and descriptive information about their reportable operating segments. The standard uses the "management approach"—segments are based on how management organizes the entity for making operating decisions and assessing performance.

**Core Principle:**
> "Segment information shall be reported on the basis that is used internally for evaluating segment performance and deciding how to allocate resources to segments."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Public business entities (SEC registrants)
- Entities required to file financial statements with SEC
- Entities that have issued debt or equity securities traded in public markets

### Does NOT Apply To:
- Private companies
- Not-for-profit organizations
- Employee benefit plans

### Interim Periods

Certain disclosures required in interim periods (less extensive than annual).

---

## Key Definitions

### Operating Segment

A component of a public entity that:

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **1. Engages in business activities** | May earn revenues and incur expenses |
| **2. Discrete financial information** | Results are regularly reviewed by CODM |
| **3. Regular review by CODM** | Chief operating decision maker uses for resource allocation and performance assessment |

### Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM)

**Definition:** A function, not necessarily a single individual, that allocates resources to and assesses the performance of operating segments.

**May be:**
- CEO
- COO
- Group of executives
- Board of directors (in some cases)

---

## Identifying Operating Segments

### Management Approach

Start with how management organizes the business:
- Review internal reporting structure
- Analyze reports provided to CODM
- Consider organizational charts

### Common Bases for Segmentation

| Basis | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Products/services** | Software segment, Hardware segment |
| **Geography** | North America, Europe, Asia |
| **Customer type** | Consumer, Commercial, Government |
| **Legal entity** | Subsidiary A, Subsidiary B |
| **Regulatory environment** | Regulated operations, Unregulated operations |

### Matrix Organization

If organization has overlapping dimensions (e.g., products AND regions):
- Identify which is primary for decision-making
- Consider which managers are held accountable
- Review how budgets and performance are assessed

---

## Aggregation Criteria

### May Aggregate Operating Segments If:

**Similar Economic Characteristics:**
Segments have similar long-term financial performance (gross margins, growth rates)

**AND All of the following are similar:**

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Nature of products/services** | What is being sold |
| **Nature of production processes** | How products are made/services delivered |
| **Type of customer** | Who buys the products/services |
| **Distribution methods** | How products reach customers |
| **Regulatory environment** | If applicable |

### Aggregation Judgment

- Requires significant judgment
- Document rationale
- Consider both current and expected future similarities

## Quantitative Thresholds

### 10% Tests

A segment is **reportable** if it meets ANY of:

| Test | Threshold |
|------|-----------|
| **Revenue test** | Segment revenue ≥ 10% of combined revenue (internal + external) of all segments |
| **Profit/loss test** | Segment profit/loss ≥ 10% of the greater of: (a) combined profit of all profitable segments, or (b) combined loss of all loss segments |
| **Asset test** | Segment assets ≥ 10% of combined assets of all segments |

### 75% Test

Reportable segments must represent at least **75% of external revenue**.

If not:
- Identify additional segments as reportable until 75% threshold met
- Management discretion on which segments to add

### Practical Limit

Generally, more than 10 segments suggests too much detail—consider aggregating.

## Quantitative Threshold Examples

### Example 1: Revenue Test

**Segment Data:**

| Segment | External Revenue | Intersegment | Total Revenue |
|---------|----------------:|-------------:|--------------:|
| A | $400,000 | $50,000 | $450,000 |
| B | $300,000 | $30,000 | $330,000 |
| C | $200,000 | $20,000 | $220,000 |
| D | $80,000 | $0 | $80,000 |
| E | $20,000 | $0 | $20,000 |
| **Total** | **$1,000,000** | **$100,000** | **$1,100,000** |

**10% Revenue Threshold:** $1,100,000 × 10% = $110,000

**Result:**
- Segment A: Reportable ($450,000 > $110,000)
- Segment B: Reportable ($330,000 > $110,000)
- Segment C: Reportable ($220,000 > $110,000)
- Segment D: Not reportable ($80,000 < $110,000)
- Segment E: Not reportable ($20,000 < $110,000)

### Example 2: Profit/Loss Test

**Segment Data:**

| Segment | Profit (Loss) |
|---------|-------------:|
| A | $100,000 |
| B | $50,000 |
| C | ($30,000) |
| D | ($80,000) |
| **Profitable total** | **$150,000** |
| **Loss total** | **($110,000)** |

**Threshold:** Greater of $150,000 or $110,000 = $150,000
**10%:** $15,000

**Result:**
- Segment A: Reportable ($100,000 > $15,000)
- Segment B: Reportable ($50,000 > $15,000)
- Segment C: Reportable ($30,000 > $15,000)
- Segment D: Reportable ($80,000 > $15,000)

## Required Disclosures

### ASU 2023-07 Enhanced Requirements (Effective 2024/2025)

**Significant Segment Expenses:**
- Disclose significant expense categories regularly provided to CODM
- Disclose expenses included in segment profit/loss
- New requirement for enhanced transparency

**CODM Information:**
- Identify title and position of CODM
- Explain how CODM uses reported measures
- Describe what measure of profit/loss represents

### General Information

| Disclosure | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Basis of organization** | How segments determined (products, geography, etc.) |
| **Products and services** | Types of products/services generating revenue |
| **CODM identification** | Title and position of CODM (ASU 2023-07) |

### Segment Profit or Loss

| Disclosure | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Revenues** | External and intersegment |
| **Significant expenses** | Categories regularly reviewed by CODM (ASU 2023-07) |
| **Interest revenue/expense** | If included in segment measure |
| **Depreciation/amortization** | If included in segment measure |
| **Other significant items** | Unusual items, equity method income |
| **Income tax expense** | If included in segment measure |
| **Segment profit/loss** | As reported to CODM |

### Segment Assets

| Disclosure | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Total assets** | By reportable segment |
| **Equity method investments** | If included in segment assets |
| **Capital expenditures** | Additions to long-lived assets |

### Reconciliations

**Required Reconciliations to Consolidated Amounts:**

| Item | Reconcile |
|------|-----------|
| Revenue | Segment revenues to consolidated revenue |
| Profit/loss | Segment profit/loss to consolidated income before tax |
| Assets | Segment assets to consolidated assets |
| Other items | Each other significant item disclosed |

## Reconciliation Example

**Segment Revenue to Consolidated Revenue:**

| | Amount |
|--|-------:|
| Segment A revenue | $450,000 |
| Segment B revenue | $330,000 |
| Segment C revenue | $220,000 |
| All other segments | $100,000 |
| Elimination of intersegment revenue | ($100,000) |
| **Consolidated revenue** | **$1,000,000** |

**Segment Profit to Consolidated Income Before Tax:**

| | Amount |
|--|-------:|
| Segment A profit | $100,000 |
| Segment B profit | $50,000 |
| Segment C loss | ($30,000) |
| All other segments loss | ($80,000) |
| Corporate expenses | ($25,000) |
| Elimination of intersegment profit | ($5,000) |
| Interest expense—corporate | ($10,000) |
| **Consolidated income before tax** | **$0** |

## Entity-Wide Disclosures

### Required for ALL Public Entities (Even Single Segment)

**Products and Services:**
- Revenue by product/service (or group)
- Unless impracticable

**Geographic Information:**
- Revenue by country (U.S. and material foreign countries)
- Long-lived assets by country
- Individually material countries separately disclosed

**Major Customers:**
- Disclose if revenue from single customer ≥ 10% of total
- Identify segment(s) reporting the revenue
- May use code names (Customer A, Customer B)
- Not required to identify customer by name

## ASU 2023-07 Changes (Effective 2024-2025)

### Key Enhancements

| Change | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Significant expenses** | Disclose significant expense categories included in segment profit/loss |
| **Other segment items** | Disclose difference between segment revenues less significant expenses and reported profit/loss |
| **CODM title** | Identify CODM by title and position |
| **CODM measure** | Describe measure of profit/loss used by CODM |
| **Interim disclosures** | Extend significant expense disclosure to interim periods |
| **Single segment** | All disclosures apply to single-segment entities |

### Effective Dates

| Entity Type | Effective Date |
|-------------|----------------|
| Public companies (annual) | Fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2023 |
| Public companies (interim) | Fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2024 |

## Measurement Principles

### Segment Information Measurement

**Use amounts reported to CODM:**
- Not required to conform to GAAP
- Allocations must be consistent with internal reporting
- Disclose measurement basis differences

### Common Differences from GAAP

| Item | Internal Measure May Differ |
|------|---------------------------|
| Pension expense | May use cash contributions |
| Stock compensation | May exclude |
| Restructuring | May exclude |
| Intercompany pricing | Transfer pricing vs. elimination |
| Interest allocation | May use different methods |

## Common Implementation Issues

### 1. CODM Identification
- Multiple individuals making different decisions
- Matrix organizations with dual reporting
- Changing organizational structure

### 2. Determining Operating Segments
- Using legal entity structure instead of management view
- Ignoring discrete financial information
- Not updating for organizational changes

### 3. Aggregation
- Insufficient documentation of similar characteristics
- Aggregating dissimilar segments
- Not reassessing aggregation annually

### 4. Quantitative Thresholds
- Calculation errors in profit/loss test
- Not retesting annually
- Missing 75% revenue test

### 5. Reconciliations
- Incomplete reconciling items
- Missing corporate/unallocated amounts
- Inconsistent allocation methods

---

## Changes in Segments

### When Segments Change

**Restatement Required:**
- Present prior period on new basis (if practicable)
- If impracticable, present current period on both bases

**Disclosure:**
- Nature of change
- Reasons for change
- Effect on segment measures
