# ASC 275: Risks and Uncertainties

> Disclosure requirements for nature of operations, significant estimates, and concentrations of risk.

## Overview

ASC 275 requires disclosure of risks and uncertainties that could significantly affect financial statements in the near term. This includes information about the nature of operations, use of estimates, and concentrations that could lead to severe impact if certain events occur.

**Core Principle:**
> "Entities shall provide disclosures about the nature of their operations, the use of estimates, and certain significant estimates and current vulnerability due to concentrations."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- All entities preparing GAAP financial statements
- Disclosure of nature of operations
- Disclosure of significant estimates
- Disclosure of concentrations

### Related Topics:
- Going concern (ASC 205-40)
- Fair value disclosures (ASC 820)
- Commitments and contingencies (ASC 440, 450)

---

## Four Categories of Disclosure

### Overview

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **1. Nature of Operations** | What the entity does |
| **2. Use of Estimates** | That estimates are used |
| **3. Certain Significant Estimates** | Estimates that could change materially |
| **4. Current Vulnerability** | Concentrations that could cause severe impact |

---

## 1. Nature of Operations

### Required Disclosure

Describe the entity's:
- Major products or services
- Principal markets (customers, geography)
- Locations of operations
- Principal business activities

### Example Disclosure

```
NOTE X: NATURE OF OPERATIONS

ABC Manufacturing, Inc. (the "Company") designs, manufactures, and
sells precision components for the aerospace and automotive
industries. The Company operates three manufacturing facilities
located in Ohio, Texas, and Mexico. Sales are made primarily to
original equipment manufacturers in North America and Europe.
Approximately 60% of sales are to the aerospace industry, with the
remaining 40% to the automotive industry.
```

---

## 2. Use of Estimates

### Required Disclosure

Statement that:
- Financial statement preparation requires estimates
- Actual results could differ from estimates

### Example Disclosure

```
USE OF ESTIMATES

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with
accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of
America requires management to make estimates and assumptions that
affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and disclosure
of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial
statements and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses during
the reporting period. Actual results could differ from those
estimates.
```

---

## 3. Certain Significant Estimates

### When to Disclose

Disclose if **BOTH** conditions are met:

| Condition | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **1. Reasonably possible** | At least reasonably possible the estimate will change |
| **2. Material effect** | Change would be material to financial statements |
| **Within near term** | Generally within one year |

### Examples of Significant Estimates

| Area | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Receivables** | Allowance for credit losses |
| **Inventory** | Obsolescence reserves |
| **Litigation** | Contingency accruals |
| **Pensions** | Actuarial assumptions |
| **Impairment** | Goodwill, long-lived assets |
| **Valuation** | Stock options, Level 3 fair values |
| **Insurance** | Self-insurance reserves |

### Disclosure Requirements

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Nature** | What the estimate relates to |
| **Range** | Possible range of outcomes (if known) |
| **Factors** | What could cause change |

### Example Disclosure

```
SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES

Allowance for Credit Losses
The allowance for credit losses is management's estimate of
expected losses inherent in accounts receivable at the balance
sheet date. The allowance is based on historical loss experience,
current conditions, and reasonable and supportable forecasts.
Actual losses could differ materially from the estimate. The
allowance for credit losses was $2.5 million at December 31, 20X1.

Litigation
The Company is a defendant in various legal proceedings. In the
opinion of management, the ultimate liability, if any, is not
expected to have a material effect on the Company's financial
position. However, the resolution of these matters is subject to
significant uncertainties.

Inventory Obsolescence
The Company maintains reserves for excess and obsolete inventory.
These reserves are based on management's estimates of future demand
and market conditions. The reserve was $1.8 million at December 31,
20X1. Changes in technology or customer demand could result in
additional inventory write-downs.
```

## 4. Current Vulnerability—Concentrations

### Types of Concentrations

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Revenue** | Major customers |
| **Supplier** | Single-source suppliers |
| **Geographic** | Operations in single area |
| **Industry** | Sales to single industry |
| **Labor** | Union concentration |
| **Credit** | Customer/counterparty credit risk |

### Disclosure Criteria

Disclose if **ALL** are met:

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **1. Concentration exists** | At date of financial statements |
| **2. Entity is vulnerable** | Could suffer severe impact |
| **3. Reasonably possible** | Event could occur in near term |
| **4. Severe impact** | Significantly disruptive |

### "Severe Impact" Definition

More than material—significantly financially disruptive:
- Bankruptcy or financial distress
- Loss of major operating capability
- Significant loss of business

## Concentration Examples

### Major Customer

```
CONCENTRATION OF CREDIT RISK

One customer accounted for approximately 35% and 32% of the
Company's net sales for the years ended December 31, 20X1 and 20X0,
respectively. At December 31, 20X1 and 20X0, this customer
represented 28% and 25% of accounts receivable, respectively. The
loss of this customer could have a material adverse effect on the
Company's operations and financial condition.
```

### Supplier Concentration

```
SUPPLIER CONCENTRATION

The Company purchases a critical component from a single supplier.
Purchases from this supplier represented approximately 45% of the
Company's total raw material purchases in 20X1. While management
believes it could obtain alternative sources for this component,
any disruption in supply could adversely impact production and
customer relationships in the near term.
```

### Geographic Concentration

```
GEOGRAPHIC CONCENTRATION

Substantially all of the Company's operations are conducted in
California. The Company's operations could be adversely affected
by natural disasters, including earthquakes, that could damage
facilities or disrupt operations.
```

### Labor Concentration

```
LABOR CONCENTRATION

Approximately 65% of the Company's employees are represented by
labor unions. The collective bargaining agreement with the primary
union expires in June 20X2. Management cannot predict the outcome
of contract negotiations. A prolonged work stoppage could
significantly impact the Company's operations.
```

### Industry Concentration

```
INDUSTRY CONCENTRATION

The Company's products are sold primarily to the oil and gas
industry, which accounted for 75% of net sales in 20X1. The
Company's results of operations are significantly influenced by
activity levels in the oil and gas industry, which in turn are
driven by commodity prices and other factors beyond the Company's
control.
```

## Comprehensive Example

```
NOTE X: RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES

Nature of Operations
XYZ Company designs and manufactures electronic components for the
telecommunications industry. The Company's products are sold
primarily to major telecommunications equipment manufacturers in
North America and Asia. The Company operates two manufacturing
facilities in the United States.

Use of Estimates
The preparation of financial statements in conformity with U.S.
GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that
affect reported amounts. Significant estimates include allowance for
credit losses, inventory reserves, warranty accruals, and the
valuation of goodwill. Actual results could differ from estimates.

Significant Estimates
Goodwill Impairment
The Company has $15 million of goodwill recorded from the 20X0
acquisition of ABC Division. Goodwill is tested annually for
impairment using a discounted cash flow analysis. Key assumptions
include revenue growth rates and discount rates. Changes in these
assumptions could result in impairment. Management performed its
annual impairment test and concluded no impairment exists; however,
continued softness in market conditions could affect future
assessments.

Warranty Reserve
The Company accrues for estimated warranty costs at the time of sale.
The warranty reserve was $3.2 million at December 31, 20X1. Actual
warranty costs could differ from estimates based on product failure
rates and repair costs.

Concentrations
Customer Concentration
Two customers accounted for 42% and 18% of net sales in 20X1. Loss
of either customer could have a material adverse effect on the
Company's business.

Supplier Concentration
The Company purchases certain electronic components from a limited
number of suppliers. Alternative sources exist but qualification
could take 6-12 months.

Geographic Concentration
Approximately 55% of net sales are to customers in Asia. Economic
conditions, trade policies, and foreign currency fluctuations could
adversely affect sales.
```

## Private Company Considerations

### Common Concentrations

| Concentration | Private Company Context |
|---------------|------------------------|
| **Customers** | Often more concentrated |
| **Owner/management** | Key person risk |
| **Suppliers** | May have fewer alternatives |
| **Geographic** | Often single location |
| **Industry** | May serve single industry |

### Additional Considerations

- Bank covenant compliance
- Key customer relationships
- Owner health/succession
- Related party dependencies

## Common Audit Issues

### Identification

| Issue | Audit Procedure |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Completeness** | Inquire about all concentrations |
| **Materiality** | Evaluate threshold |
| **Near-term** | Define time horizon |
| **Severity** | Assess potential impact |

### Disclosure Quality

| Issue | Focus |
|-------|-------|
| **Specificity** | Enough detail for users |
| **Accuracy** | Percentages and amounts correct |
| **Consistency** | Matches financial data |
| **Updates** | Current year circumstances |
