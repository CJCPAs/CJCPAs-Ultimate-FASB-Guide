# ASC 230: Statement of Cash Flows

> The comprehensive standard for preparing and presenting the statement of cash flows.

## Overview

ASC 230 establishes standards for the statement of cash flows, one of the primary financial statements. The statement reports cash receipts and payments during a period, classified by operating, investing, and financing activities.

**Core Principle:**
> "A statement of cash flows shall explain the change during the period in cash and cash equivalents, classifying cash flows by operating, investing, and financing activities."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- All entities presenting a full set of financial statements
- Both business entities and not-for-profit organizations

### Exemptions:
- Defined benefit pension plans (ASC 960)
- Certain investment companies (ASC 946)
- Highly liquid investment funds meeting specific criteria

---

## Cash and Cash Equivalents

### Definition

**Cash:** Currency on hand, demand deposits, and other accounts with characteristics of demand deposits.

**Cash Equivalents:** Short-term, highly liquid investments that are:
- Readily convertible to known amounts of cash
- So near maturity that they present insignificant risk of value change
- Generally, original maturity of **three months or less**

### Examples

| Cash Equivalents | NOT Cash Equivalents |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Treasury bills (≤3 months) | Treasury bills (>3 months) |
| Commercial paper (≤3 months) | Equity securities |
| Money market funds | Restricted cash (may require separate presentation) |
| Bank CDs (≤3 months) | CDs >3 months |

### Restricted Cash

**ASU 2016-18 Requirements:**
- Include restricted cash/cash equivalents with cash in the reconciliation
- Provide reconciliation if balance sheet presents restricted cash separately
- Disclose nature and amount of restrictions

## Classification of Cash Flows

### Operating Activities

**Definition:** Cash flows from the entity's principal revenue-producing activities and other activities not classified as investing or financing.

| Cash Inflows | Cash Outflows |
|--------------|---------------|
| Cash receipts from customers | Payments to suppliers |
| Cash receipts from interest/dividends | Payments to employees |
| Insurance proceeds (operating) | Payments for taxes |
| Lawsuit settlements (operating) | Payments for interest |
| Refunds from suppliers | Payments for insurance |

### Investing Activities

**Definition:** Cash flows from acquiring and disposing of long-term assets and other investments not included in cash equivalents.

| Cash Inflows | Cash Outflows |
|--------------|---------------|
| Sale of PP&E | Purchase of PP&E |
| Sale of investments | Purchase of investments |
| Collection of loans made | Loans made to others |
| Sale of business segment | Acquisition of businesses |
| Insurance proceeds (casualty loss on PP&E) | Capitalized software costs |

### Financing Activities

**Definition:** Cash flows from activities that result in changes in equity capital and borrowings.

| Cash Inflows | Cash Outflows |
|--------------|---------------|
| Issuance of stock | Repurchase of treasury stock |
| Proceeds from debt | Repayment of debt |
| Proceeds from credit facility | Debt issuance costs |
| | Dividends paid |
| | Finance lease payments (principal) |

## Methods of Presentation

### Direct Method (Preferred)

Reports major classes of gross cash receipts and payments.

**Format:**
```
Cash flows from operating activities:
  Cash received from customers                    $XXX
  Cash paid to suppliers                         (XXX)
  Cash paid to employees                         (XXX)
  Interest paid                                  (XXX)
  Income taxes paid                              (XXX)
  Net cash provided by operating activities      $XXX
```

**Advantage:** More informative—shows actual cash flows
**Disadvantage:** Requires detailed records of cash transactions

### Indirect Method (More Common)

Starts with net income and adjusts for non-cash items and changes in working capital.

**Format:**
```
Cash flows from operating activities:
  Net income                                     $XXX
  Adjustments to reconcile net income to cash:
    Depreciation and amortization                 XXX
    Gain on sale of equipment                    (XXX)
    Loss on impairment                            XXX
    Deferred income taxes                         XXX
    Changes in operating assets and liabilities:
      (Increase) decrease in accounts receivable (XXX)
      (Increase) decrease in inventory           (XXX)
      Increase (decrease) in accounts payable     XXX
      Increase (decrease) in accrued expenses     XXX
  Net cash provided by operating activities      $XXX
```

### Reconciliation Requirement

If using direct method, must also provide reconciliation of net income to operating cash flows (effectively showing indirect method as supplementary).

## Common Adjustments (Indirect Method)

### Add to Net Income

| Item | Reason |
|------|--------|
| Depreciation and amortization | Non-cash expense |
| Impairment losses | Non-cash expense |
| Losses on disposal of assets | Non-operating, or non-cash portion |
| Amortization of bond discount | Non-cash interest expense |
| Stock-based compensation | Non-cash expense |
| Deferred tax expense | Non-cash (timing difference) |
| Decrease in operating assets | Cash received exceeded revenue |
| Increase in operating liabilities | Cash paid less than expense |

### Subtract from Net Income

| Item | Reason |
|------|--------|
| Gains on disposal of assets | Non-operating, or non-cash portion |
| Amortization of bond premium | Reduces cash interest paid |
| Equity in earnings of investee (undistributed) | Non-cash income |
| Deferred tax benefit | Non-cash |
| Increase in operating assets | Cash paid exceeded expense |
| Decrease in operating liabilities | Cash paid exceeded expense |

---

## Working Capital Changes

### General Rules

| Change | Effect on Cash |
|--------|----------------|
| **Asset increases** | Cash outflow (subtract) |
| **Asset decreases** | Cash inflow (add) |
| **Liability increases** | Cash inflow (add) |
| **Liability decreases** | Cash outflow (subtract) |

### Common Working Capital Items

| Item | Increase → | Decrease → |
|------|------------|------------|
| Accounts receivable | Subtract (collected less than earned) | Add (collected more than earned) |
| Inventory | Subtract (purchased more than sold) | Add (sold more than purchased) |
| Prepaid expenses | Subtract (paid more than expensed) | Add (expensed more than paid) |
| Accounts payable | Add (paid less than expense) | Subtract (paid more than expense) |
| Accrued liabilities | Add (expensed more than paid) | Subtract (paid more than expensed) |
| Deferred revenue | Add (collected more than earned) | Subtract (earned more than collected) |

## Special Topics

### Interest and Dividends

| Item | Classification |
|------|----------------|
| Interest received | Operating |
| Dividends received | Operating |
| Interest paid | Operating |
| Dividends paid | **Financing** |

**Note:** Interest paid may be classified as financing under IFRS, but must be operating under U.S. GAAP.

### Income Taxes

- Classify based on predominant activity generating the tax
- If impractical to allocate, classify as operating
- Disclose total income taxes paid

### Non-Cash Investing and Financing

**Do not include in statement** but **disclose separately:**

| Example | Disclosure Required |
|---------|---------------------|
| Conversion of debt to equity | Yes |
| Acquisition via stock issuance | Yes |
| Capital lease asset/liability recognition | Yes |
| Exchange of non-cash assets | Yes |
| Dividends declared but not paid | Yes |

### Business Combinations

| Cash Flow | Classification |
|-----------|----------------|
| Cash paid for acquisition | Investing (net of cash acquired) |
| Acquisition-related costs | Operating |
| Contingent consideration paid | Financing (up to acquisition-date fair value), Operating (excess) |

### Debt

| Transaction | Classification |
|-------------|----------------|
| Proceeds from issuance | Financing |
| Principal payments | Financing |
| Debt issuance costs | Financing |
| Interest payments | Operating |
| Refinancing (net or gross) | Financing |

### Leases (ASC 842)

| Payment Type | Lessee Classification |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Operating lease—fixed payments | Operating |
| Finance lease—principal | Financing |
| Finance lease—interest | Operating |
| Short-term lease payments | Operating |

## Gross vs. Net Presentation

### General Rule

Present cash flows **gross** (not netted).

### Exceptions (May Net)

Certain items may be presented net if:
- Turnover is quick
- Amounts are large
- Maturities are short

| May Present Net |
|-----------------|
| Investments in highly liquid securities |
| Loans receivable (if demand deposits) |
| Debt with maturities ≤3 months |

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Indirect Method Preparation

**Given Information:**
- Net income: $500,000
- Depreciation: $75,000
- Amortization of intangibles: $25,000
- Gain on sale of equipment: $10,000
- Increase in accounts receivable: $30,000
- Decrease in inventory: $15,000
- Increase in accounts payable: $20,000
- Decrease in accrued wages: $5,000

**Operating Cash Flow Calculation:**

| Item | Amount |
|------|-------:|
| Net income | $500,000 |
| Add: Depreciation | 75,000 |
| Add: Amortization | 25,000 |
| Less: Gain on sale of equipment | (10,000) |
| Less: Increase in accounts receivable | (30,000) |
| Add: Decrease in inventory | 15,000 |
| Add: Increase in accounts payable | 20,000 |
| Less: Decrease in accrued wages | (5,000) |
| **Net cash from operating activities** | **$590,000** |

---

### Example 2: Classification Scenarios

**Scenario A: Sale of Equipment**
- Sold equipment with book value of $50,000 for $65,000 cash

**Classification:**
- Cash received ($65,000): Investing activity
- Gain ($15,000): Remove from operating (indirect method)

**Indirect Method Adjustment:**
```
Net income includes: $15,000 gain
Less: Gain on sale: ($15,000)  ← Remove from operating
```

**Investing Activities:**
```
Proceeds from sale of equipment: $65,000
```

---

**Scenario B: Bond Issuance with Discount**
- Issued $1,000,000 bonds at 98 (received $980,000)
- Bond discount: $20,000

**Classification:**
```
Financing Activities:
  Proceeds from issuance of bonds: $980,000
```

**Note:** The discount is not a separate cash flow; it reduces proceeds.

---

**Scenario C: Convertible Debt Conversion**
- Bondholders converted $500,000 bonds to common stock
- No cash exchanged

**Classification:** Non-cash transaction

**Disclosure:**
> During the year, bondholders converted $500,000 of convertible bonds into 50,000 shares of common stock.

## Free Cash Flow (Non-GAAP)

### Common Calculation

**Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow − Capital Expenditures**

### Variations

| Measure | Calculation |
|---------|-------------|
| **FCF (basic)** | Operating CF − CapEx |
| **FCF to Firm** | Operating CF + Interest(1-t) − CapEx |
| **FCF to Equity** | Operating CF − CapEx − Debt payments + Debt proceeds |

**Note:** Free cash flow is a non-GAAP measure; if presented, must reconcile to GAAP.

## Disclosure Requirements

### Required Disclosures

| Disclosure | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Accounting policy** | Definition of cash equivalents |
| **Interest paid** | Total cash paid for interest |
| **Taxes paid** | Total cash paid for income taxes |
| **Non-cash activities** | Investing and financing activities not involving cash |
| **Restricted cash** | Reconciliation to balance sheet amounts |

### Restricted Cash Reconciliation

If restricted cash presented separately on balance sheet:

```
Cash and cash equivalents                    $500,000
Restricted cash—current                        50,000
Restricted cash—noncurrent                    100,000
Total cash, cash equivalents, and
  restricted cash (per cash flow statement)  $650,000
```

## Common Implementation Issues

### 1. Classification Errors
- Interest paid classified as financing (should be operating)
- Dividends received classified as financing (should be operating)
- Acquisition costs classified as investing (should be operating)

### 2. Working Capital Adjustments
- Forgetting to adjust for non-cash items in working capital
- Double-counting items
- Missing reclassifications

### 3. Non-Cash Transactions
- Including non-cash items in the statement
- Missing required supplemental disclosures
- Incomplete disclosure of terms

### 4. Restricted Cash
- Not including in cash reconciliation
- Missing separate disclosure

### 5. Business Combinations
- Gross vs. net of cash acquired
- Classification of contingent consideration
- Treatment of acquisition costs

## Preparing Cash Flow Statement—Checklist

### Operating Activities (Indirect Method)

- [ ] Start with net income
- [ ] Add back depreciation and amortization
- [ ] Add back impairment losses
- [ ] Remove gains (add back losses) on disposals
- [ ] Adjust for deferred taxes
- [ ] Add stock-based compensation
- [ ] Analyze all working capital accounts
- [ ] Exclude non-cash working capital changes
- [ ] Verify total equals change in cash

### Investing Activities

- [ ] PP&E purchases (check for non-cash)
- [ ] PP&E sales (remove gain/loss from operating)
- [ ] Investment purchases and sales
- [ ] Loans made and collected
- [ ] Acquisitions (net of cash acquired)
- [ ] Capitalized costs

### Financing Activities

- [ ] Debt proceeds and payments
- [ ] Stock issuances and repurchases
- [ ] Dividend payments
- [ ] Debt issuance costs
- [ ] Finance lease payments (principal only)

### Supplemental Disclosures

- [ ] Interest paid
- [ ] Taxes paid
- [ ] Non-cash investing and financing activities
- [ ] Restricted cash reconciliation
