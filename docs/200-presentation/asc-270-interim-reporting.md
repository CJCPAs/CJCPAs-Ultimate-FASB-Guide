# ASC 270: Interim Reporting

> Guidance on preparing and presenting financial information for periods shorter than a full fiscal year.

## Overview

ASC 270 establishes the principles for preparing interim financial reports, including quarterly financial statements. The fundamental approach is that each interim period should be viewed as an integral part of an annual period, with certain exceptions for discrete-period treatment.

**Core Principle:**
> "Each interim period shall be viewed primarily as an integral part of an annual period, and the principles for measuring interim period results should produce an interim report that reflects reasonably foreseeable annual results."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- Quarterly financial statements
- Monthly financial statements
- Any period less than a full fiscal year
- SEC registrants and private companies

### Related Guidance:
- SEC Regulation S-X Article 10
- SEC Form 10-Q requirements
- ASC 250 (Accounting Changes)
- ASC 740 (Income Taxes)

## Key Concepts

### Integral vs. Discrete Approach

| Approach | Description | Application |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Integral** | Interim period is part of annual period | Most items |
| **Discrete** | Interim period stands alone | Unusual/infrequent items |

### When to Use Discrete Treatment

| Item | Treatment |
|------|-----------|
| Extraordinary items (historical) | Discrete |
| Unusual or infrequent items | Discrete |
| Discontinued operations | Discrete |
| Changes in accounting principle | Discrete |
| Litigation settlements | Discrete |

## Revenue Recognition

### General Principle

Revenue is recognized in interim periods on the same basis as annual periods:

| Approach | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **ASC 606 five-step model** | Apply each interim period |
| **Consistent policies** | Same as annual |
| **No special interim rules** | Recognize when earned |

### Seasonal Considerations

| Issue | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|
| **Seasonal revenue** | Recognize in period earned; do not anticipate |
| **Seasonal costs** | May defer/accrue if benefit all periods |
| **Disclosure** | Note seasonal nature of business |

---

## Cost of Sales

### Inventory Valuation

| Method | Interim Treatment |
|--------|-------------------|
| **LIFO** | May use estimate if costs expected to be replaced |
| **LIFO liquidation** | Consider if temporary |
| **Lower of cost or NRV** | Apply each period; may reverse |

### LIFO Liquidation

If LIFO layer is liquidated at interim but expected to be replaced by year-end:
- Do NOT recognize income from liquidation at interim
- Charge COGS at replacement cost

```
Dr. Cost of Goods Sold (at replacement)    $XXX
    Cr. Inventory (at LIFO cost)                 $XXX
    Cr. Excess of Replacement over LIFO          $XXX
```

### Inventory Market Declines

| Timing | Treatment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Decline at interim** | Recognize loss |
| **Recovery before year-end** | Recover loss (limited to original write-down) |
| **Recovery after year-end** | No recovery in interim |

---

## Operating Expenses

### General Principle

Expenses that benefit multiple periods may be allocated:

| Expense Type | Treatment |
|--------------|-----------|
| **Directly attributable** | Recognize in period incurred |
| **Benefit entire year** | Allocate ratably |
| **Variable costs** | Recognize as incurred |

### Examples

| Expense | Interim Treatment |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Property taxes** | Accrue ratably over year |
| **Major repairs (annual)** | Accrue or defer if benefits all periods |
| **Advertising** | Expense as incurred (generally) |
| **Bonuses (annual)** | Accrue ratably if determinable |
| **Depreciation** | Recognize each period |

### Advertising Costs

| Situation | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Benefits extend beyond interim** | May defer to future periods |
| **Consumed in period** | Expense as incurred |
| **Unclear benefit** | Expense as incurred (conservative) |

## Income Taxes

### Estimated Annual Effective Tax Rate (EAETR)

**Step 1:** Estimate annual ordinary income
**Step 2:** Estimate annual tax expense
**Step 3:** Calculate estimated annual effective rate
**Step 4:** Apply to interim ordinary income

### Calculation

```
EAETR = Estimated Annual Tax / Estimated Annual Ordinary Income

Interim Tax = Cumulative Ordinary Income × EAETR - Prior Period Tax
```

### Example

**Facts:**
- Q1 ordinary income: $1,000,000
- Estimated annual income: $4,000,000
- Estimated annual tax: $1,000,000
- EAETR: 25%

**Q1 Tax Provision:**
```
Q1 Tax = $1,000,000 × 25% = $250,000
```

**Q2 (cumulative income $2,500,000):**
```
Cumulative tax = $2,500,000 × 25% = $625,000
Less: Q1 tax = ($250,000)
Q2 tax provision = $375,000
```

### Discrete Items

Items taxed/benefited discretely (not through EAETR):

| Item | Treatment |
|------|-----------|
| Unusual or infrequent items | Tax in period occurred |
| Discontinued operations | Tax separately |
| Certain tax credits | May be discrete |
| Valuation allowance changes | Evaluate each period |
| Rate changes | Discrete to period enacted |

## Accounting Changes

### Changes in Accounting Principle

| Type | Treatment |
|------|-----------|
| **Retrospective** | Restate prior interim periods presented |
| **Cumulative effect** | Record in first interim period |
| **Prospective** | Apply from date of change |

### Changes in Estimate

```
Recognized prospectively in period of change
```

### Error Corrections

| Discovery | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **In subsequent interim period** | Restate prior interim periods |
| **After year-end** | Restate annual financial statements |

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Quarterly Property Tax Accrual

**Facts:**
- Annual property tax: $120,000
- Tax year: Calendar year
- Payment due: November

**Quarterly Accrual:**
```
Dr. Property Tax Expense              $30,000
    Cr. Accrued Property Tax                   $30,000
(Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 accrual)
```

### Example 2: Annual Bonus Accrual

**Facts:**
- Estimated annual bonus pool: $400,000
- Bonuses based on annual performance
- Paid in February following year

**Quarterly Accrual:**
```
Dr. Bonus Expense                    $100,000
    Cr. Accrued Bonus                        $100,000
(Assuming consistent estimate each quarter)
```

### Example 3: Inventory Write-Down Recovery

**Q2—Inventory write-down:**
```
Dr. Cost of Goods Sold               $50,000
    Cr. Inventory                             $50,000
```

**Q3—Market price recovers:**
```
Dr. Inventory                        $50,000
    Cr. Cost of Goods Sold                    $50,000
(Recovery limited to original write-down)
```

## Private Company Considerations

### Simplified Approach

Private companies may:
- Not prepare interim financial statements
- Use simplified estimates
- Provide limited disclosures
- Apply bank reporting requirements

### Common Private Company Interim Issues

| Issue | Consideration |
|-------|---------------|
| **Bank requirements** | Monthly/quarterly reporting |
| **Bonding** | Periodic financial updates |
| **Tax estimates** | Quarterly estimated payments |
| **Internal management** | Monthly/quarterly analysis |

## Comparison: Interim vs. Annual

| Item | Interim | Annual |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Revenue** | Same GAAP | Same GAAP |
| **COGS** | Special LIFO rules | Standard |
| **Expenses** | May allocate | Recognize as incurred |
| **Income taxes** | EAETR method | Full year calculation |
| **Disclosures** | Condensed | Complete |
| **Seasonal items** | Note seasonality | Full year results |
