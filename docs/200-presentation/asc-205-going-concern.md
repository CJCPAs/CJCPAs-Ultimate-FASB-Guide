# ASC 205-40: Going Concern

> The comprehensive standard for evaluating and disclosing an entity's ability to continue as a going concern.

## Overview

ASC 205-40 (formerly ASU 2014-15) provides guidance on management's responsibility to evaluate whether there is substantial doubt about an entity's ability to continue as a going concern and to provide related disclosures. This standard shifted responsibility from auditors to management and established clear criteria for evaluation and disclosure.

**Core Principle:**
> "Management should evaluate whether there are conditions or events that raise substantial doubt about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern within one year after the financial statements are issued."

---

## Scope

### Applies To:
- All entities preparing GAAP financial statements
- Both public and private companies
- Not-for-profit organizations
- Employee benefit plans

### Specific Guidance:
- ASC 205-40 applies to all entities
- Additional SEC guidance for public companies
- PCAOB standards for auditor responsibilities

---

## Key Definitions

### Substantial Doubt

**Substantial doubt exists when:**
Conditions or events, considered in the aggregate, indicate it is **probable** that the entity will be unable to meet its obligations as they become due within one year after the financial statement issuance date.

### Probable

Consistent with ASC 450 (Contingencies):
- The future event is **likely to occur**
- Higher threshold than "more likely than not"

### One-Year Period

| Entity Type | Measurement Date |
|-------------|------------------|
| **SEC filers** | One year from issuance date |
| **Non-SEC filers** | One year from date available to be issued |

## Evaluation Framework

### Step 1: Identify Conditions or Events

**Common Indicators:**

| Category | Indicators |
|----------|------------|
| **Financial** | Recurring losses, negative cash flows, working capital deficiency |
| **Operating** | Loss of key customer, labor difficulties, supply chain issues |
| **External** | Legal proceedings, regulatory actions, loss of license |
| **Debt-related** | Debt defaults, covenant violations, inability to refinance |
| **Other** | Catastrophic events, loss of key personnel |

### Step 2: Assess Probability

**Consider in the aggregate:**
- All known conditions and events
- Their interrelationship
- Historical patterns
- Future projections

### Step 3: Evaluate Management's Plans

If substantial doubt exists before considering management's plans:
- Evaluate feasibility and effectiveness of plans
- Consider only plans that are:
  - Approved by appropriate level of management
  - Probable of being implemented
  - Probable of mitigating the conditions

### Step 4: Determine Disclosure Requirements

**Decision Tree:**

```
Is there substantial doubt about going concern?
│
├── NO (before considering plans)
│   └── No disclosure required
│
└── YES (before considering plans)
    │
    ├── Management's plans MITIGATE substantial doubt
    │   └── Disclose: Principal conditions + Management's plans
    │
    └── Management's plans DO NOT MITIGATE
        └── Disclose: Substantial doubt exists + Plans
            (Going concern basis may still be appropriate)
```

---

## Management's Plans

### Types of Mitigating Plans

| Plan Category | Examples |
|---------------|----------|
| **Financing** | Obtain new debt/equity, refinance existing debt |
| **Operating** | Reduce costs, delay capital expenditures, sell assets |
| **Restructuring** | Merge with another entity, restructure debt |
| **Other** | Obtain additional capital from owners |

### Evaluating Feasibility

**Plan is feasible when:**
- Has been approved
- Can be executed within evaluation period
- Will generate sufficient cash flows

### Evaluating Probability of Implementation

| Factor | Consideration |
|--------|---------------|
| **Historical track record** | Has management successfully executed similar plans? |
| **Committed financing** | Are financing sources identified and committed? |
| **Board/owner approval** | Required approvals obtained? |
| **Execution timeline** | Can plans be implemented in time? |

### Probability of Mitigating

Will the plan, if implemented, actually alleviate the conditions?

**Consider:**
- Cash flow impact and timing
- Effect on operations
- Likelihood of success
- Interdependencies between plans

## Disclosure Requirements

### Scenario 1: Substantial Doubt Alleviated by Management's Plans

**Required Disclosures:**

| Disclosure |
|------------|
| Principal conditions or events raising substantial doubt |
| Management's evaluation of significance |
| Management's plans that alleviated the doubt |

### Scenario 2: Substantial Doubt NOT Alleviated

**Required Disclosures:**

| Disclosure |
|------------|
| Statement that substantial doubt exists |
| Principal conditions or events |
| Management's evaluation of significance |
| Management's plans (even if not sufficient) |

### Disclosure Location

- Typically in notes to financial statements
- May warrant emphasis paragraph in auditor's report
- SEC filings may require MD&A discussion

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Substantial Doubt Alleviated

**Facts:**
- Company has negative working capital of $2 million
- Recurring operating losses for 3 years
- Significant debt maturing in 8 months
- Company has signed commitment letter for refinancing
- Lender has approved terms; closing expected in 2 months

**Analysis:**
- Conditions exist that raise substantial doubt
- Management's plan: Refinance maturing debt
- Plan is probable of implementation (commitment letter signed)
- Plan will alleviate conditions (provides liquidity)

**Disclosure (Note):**

*Going Concern*

*The Company has experienced recurring losses from operations and has a working capital deficiency of $2.0 million at December 31, 20XX. Additionally, the Company has $5.0 million of debt maturing in August 20XY. These conditions raised substantial doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a going concern.*

*Management has obtained a commitment letter from ABC Bank to refinance the maturing debt with a new 5-year term loan. The refinancing is expected to close in February 20XY. Based on management's plans, the Company believes it will have sufficient liquidity to meet its obligations as they become due over the next twelve months.*

---

### Example 2: Substantial Doubt NOT Alleviated

**Facts:**
- Startup company with no revenue
- Cash runway of only 6 months
- Seeking Series B funding but no commitments
- Has reduced burn rate by 20%

**Analysis:**
- Conditions exist (limited cash runway)
- Management's plan: Seek additional funding
- Plan not yet probable (no commitments)
- Substantial doubt not alleviated

**Disclosure (Note):**

*Going Concern*

*The accompanying financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis, which contemplates the realization of assets and satisfaction of liabilities in the normal course of business.*

*The Company has incurred net losses since inception and has limited cash resources. As of December 31, 20XX, the Company has cash of $3.0 million, which management estimates will fund operations for approximately six months. These conditions raise substantial doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a going concern.*

*Management is pursuing additional equity financing and has reduced operating expenses by 20%. However, there can be no assurance that the Company will be successful in raising additional capital. The financial statements do not include any adjustments that might result from the outcome of this uncertainty.*

---

### Example 3: Covenant Violation

**Facts:**
- Company violated debt covenant at year-end
- Debt classified as current ($10 million)
- Working capital now negative
- Bank issued waiver for 90 days

**Analysis:**
- Covenant violation creates uncertainty
- 90-day waiver does not extend beyond evaluation period
- Management plans to cure through asset sale

**Considerations:**
- Is asset sale probable?
- Will proceeds cure the violation?
- Timeline for sale completion?

## Decision Matrix

| Conditions Present? | Plans Mitigate? | Disclosure Requirement |
|--------------------:|:---------------:|:-----------------------|
| No | N/A | None |
| Yes | Yes | Conditions + Plans |
| Yes | No | Substantial doubt statement + Conditions + Plans |

## Timing Considerations

### Evaluation Frequency

| Event | Action Required |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Annual reporting** | Evaluate at each balance sheet date |
| **Interim reporting** | Evaluate if conditions change |
| **Subsequent events** | Consider through issuance date |
| **Reissuance** | Re-evaluate if financial statements reissued |

### Subsequent Events

**Type I (Recognized):**
- Conditions existing at balance sheet date
- Consider in going concern evaluation

**Type II (Non-recognized):**
- Conditions arising after balance sheet date
- May require disclosure

## Financial Statement Implications

### When Going Concern Basis Appropriate

Even with substantial doubt, going concern basis usually appropriate:
- Management believes entity can continue
- Plans may still be executed
- Liquidation not imminent

### When Liquidation Basis Required

Apply ASC 205-30 (Liquidation Basis) when:
- Liquidation is imminent
- Court-approved liquidation plan
- Management-approved liquidation plan with no realistic alternative

### Classification Effects

Substantial doubt may affect:
- Current vs. noncurrent debt classification
- Asset impairment assessments
- Deferred tax asset valuation

## Auditor Considerations

### Auditor's Responsibilities

| Responsibility |
|---------------|
| Evaluate management's assessment |
| Conclude on appropriateness of going concern basis |
| Evaluate adequacy of disclosures |
| Consider report modifications |

### Report Modifications

| Situation | Report Impact |
|-----------|---------------|
| Substantial doubt alleviated | No modification required |
| Substantial doubt exists | Emphasis-of-matter paragraph |
| Inadequate disclosure | Qualified or adverse opinion |
| Liquidation appropriate but not used | Adverse opinion |

## Private Company Considerations

### Practical Challenges

| Challenge | Consideration |
|-----------|---------------|
| Owner support | Document intent and ability |
| Related party financing | Evaluate probability and terms |
| Limited projections | May need external assistance |
| Informal arrangements | Formalize commitments |

### Common Owner/Shareholder Support

**Acceptable Mitigation:**
- Written commitment to provide capital
- Subordination of related-party debt
- Personal guarantee of entity debt

**Documentation Needed:**
- Written agreement
- Evidence of ability to fund
- Legal enforceability

## Common Implementation Issues

### 1. Evaluation Period
- Using wrong measurement date
- Not extending to full one-year period
- Ignoring interim changes

### 2. Plan Probability
- Counting on plans not yet approved
- Overestimating ability to execute
- Ignoring historical performance

### 3. Disclosure Adequacy
- Boilerplate language without specifics
- Missing required elements
- Inconsistent with MD&A

### 4. Subsequent Events
- Not updating through issuance date
- Missing material changes
- Inadequate disclosure of new information

### 5. Objectivity
- Optimism bias in projections
- Underestimating risks
- Overreliance on management intentions

## Documentation Best Practices

### Management's Evaluation Should Document:

| Element |
|---------|
| Conditions or events considered |
| Quantified impact of conditions |
| Cash flow projections and assumptions |
| Management's plans in detail |
| Probability assessment of each plan |
| Conclusion and basis |
| Approval by appropriate management level |

### Supporting Evidence

| Evidence Type |
|---------------|
| Board meeting minutes |
| Commitment letters |
| Cash flow forecasts |
| Correspondence with lenders |
| Legal opinions (if applicable) |

## Comparison: U.S. GAAP vs. IFRS

| Aspect | U.S. GAAP (ASC 205-40) | IFRS (IAS 1) |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Who evaluates** | Management | Management |
| **Time period** | 1 year from issuance | At least 12 months |
| **Probability threshold** | "Probable" | "Material uncertainty" |
| **Disclosure trigger** | Substantial doubt | Material uncertainty |
